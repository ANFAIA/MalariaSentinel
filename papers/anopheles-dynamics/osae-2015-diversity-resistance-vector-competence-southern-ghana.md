# Diversity, resistance and vector competence of endophilic anophelines from southern Ghana

**Authors:** Michael Osae, Alex Kwawukume, Michael Wilson, David Wilson, Lynn Koekemoer
**Journal:** MalariaWorld Journal | **Year:** 2015 (vol. 6) | **DOI:** 10.5281/zenodo.10876351
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/osae-2015-diversity-resistance-vector-competence-southern-ghana.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Baseline entomological study of the endophilic anophelines of southern Ghana.
Indoor resting anophelines were collected by mouth aspirator and pyrethroid
spray catch in six villages covering three ecological zones (forest, forest
savannah transition, coastal savannah). Females were identified morphologically
and sibling species resolved by PCR; *kdr* L1014F was assayed by TaqMan
RT-PCR; blood-meal sources by multiplex PCR; *Plasmodium falciparum*
circumsporozoite protein by ELISA.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Species composition:** 892 female *Anopheles* collected — **31 % *An.
  gambiae*, 41 % *An. coluzzii*, 28 % *An. funestus***. Of 308 female
  *An. gambiae* s.l. processed by PCR: **176 (57.71 %) *An. coluzzii*** and
  132 (42.86 %) *An. gambiae* s.s. In the **coastal savannah zone** the
  collections from Okyereko and Adawukwa were **exclusively *An. coluzzii***
  (irrigated rice fields and swamp/stream) — direct support for a coluzzii
  pilot in the Greater Accra coastal belt.
- **Anthropophagy / HBI:** *An. funestus* was exclusively anthropophagic
  (**HBI = 1**). *An. gambiae* s.s. and *An. coluzzii* were "similarly highly
  anthropophagic"; evidence of animal blood feeding was recorded in both, but
  **every animal-fed specimen had also fed on humans, except one *An.
  coluzzii*** female from Okyereko that fed exclusively on cow blood. The
  anthropophilic index for *An. coluzzii* in southern Ghana is therefore
  ≈ 1 (no numeric HBI is reported for the species).
- **Vector competence (CSP rate, ELISA):** *P. falciparum* detected in all
  three vectors — **4.9 % in *An. funestus*, 3.8 % in *An. gambiae* s.s.,
  1.1 % in *An. coluzzii***.
- **Insecticide resistance:** *kdr* L1014F near-fixation — *An. coluzzii*
  allele frequency **0.75 (Osorongma) to 0.90 (Agyenkwaso)**, 0.87 (Adawukwa),
  0.86 (Okyereko); *An. gambiae* s.s. **0.97–1.00**.
- **Larval-habitat association (used for habitat preference):** *An. coluzzii*
  "tends to be associated with flooded or irrigated sites that provide
  permanent breeding conditions, whereas *An. gambiae* s.s. is associated with
  rain-dependent temporary sites". Rice fields around Okyereko and the swamp
  and stream around Adawukwa are described as ideal *An. coluzzii* breeding
  sites; temporary puddles in clay soils around Atatam / Agyenkwaso favour
  *An. gambiae* s.s.
- **Distance of breeding sites to houses:** cites Charlwood & Edoh (1986):
  anopheline adult density is **negatively correlated with the distance
  between larval habitats and houses**; the complete absence of *An. funestus*
  in Osorongma is explained by the absence of permanent water bodies near the
  village.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Sole source for a measured **human blood index for Ghanaian *An. coluzzii*
(≈ 1, essentially anthropophilic)** — the closest empirical support for
`species.coluzzii.pref.human = 0.99` and for the anthropophily assumption of
the host-seeking layer. Also the source of the **coastal-savannah dominance of
*An. coluzzii*** and of the **permanent (irrigated) vs temporary (rain-fed)
habitat dichotomy** that the `weights` row of the YAML encodes.
**Caveats:** collections are indoor-resting (endophilic) only, so densities and
composition are biased toward endophagic behaviour; the paper reports no
*An. coluzzii* HBI number and no biting rate.
