# Mating activates the heme peroxidase HPX15 in the sperm storage organ to ensure fertility in *Anopheles gambiae*

**Authors:** Shaw WR, Teodori E, Mitchell SN, Baldini F, Gabrieli P, Rogers DW, Catteruccia F
**Journal:** PNAS | **Year:** 2014 (111(16):5854–5859) | **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.1401715111
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/shaw-2014-gambiae-spermatheca-hpx15-fertility.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Molecular study of the *Anopheles gambiae* spermatheca. It establishes the two
biological facts on which any male-aggregation decision rests: (i) *A. gambiae*
females are essentially **monandrous** and must use the sperm stored in the
spermatheca to fertilise a lifetime's supply of eggs; (ii) the maintenance of sperm
viability during storage is actively regulated by the female (via the mating-induced
heme peroxidase HPX15, itself induced by 20-hydroxyecdysone transferred in the male
accessory-gland secretions).

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- "**Unlike many other insects, *Anopheles gambiae* females mate a single time in
  their lives and must use sperm stored in the sperm storage organ, the spermatheca,
  to fertilize a lifetime's supply of eggs.** Maintenance of sperm viability during
  storage is therefore crucial to the reproductive capacity of these mosquitoes."
- "Pathways of energy metabolism, cellular transport, and oxidative stress are
  strongly regulated by mating in the spermatheca."
- "We identify the **mating-induced heme peroxidase (HPX) 15** as an important factor
  in long-term fertility, and demonstrate that **its function is required during
  multiple gonotrophic cycles**."
- HPX15 induction is regulated by **sexually transferred 20-hydroxyecdysone (20E)**, a
  steroid hormone produced by the **male accessory glands** and transferred during
  copulation; expression is mediated via the 20E nuclear receptor.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This is the mechanistic statement of **why a single insemination can be enough**, and
therefore why a per-female insemination probability (option A) is a *biologically
legitimate* aggregate rather than a crude shortcut:

- One successful mating supplies the sperm for the female's **whole lifetime**
  (typically only 1–2 egg batches in *A. coluzzii*), so the male's contribution is a
  **one-shot fertilisation**, not a continuous provisioning.
- The female actively **maintains and curates** the stored sperm (HPX15, oxidative-
  stress control) — i.e. sperm quality is a female-side property, not something the
  male module needs to track day by day.
- Therefore the male's *epidemiological* contribution to the Centinela reduces to:
  **P(female is inseminated before her first blood meal)**, and to the extent that
  sperm quantity matters (Ekechukwu & Tripet 2019: 1 700–7 000 cells stored), a
  per-female "fertility weight" that is **1 if inseminated, 0 if not**.
