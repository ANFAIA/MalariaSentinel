# Sex ratios influence spatial occupancy and kinematic stability of Anopheles coluzzii mosquito swarms

**Authors:** Svenja Vielma, Sawadogo S., Ouédraogo T., Cribellier A., Muijres F., Diabate A., Müller R.
**Journal:** Parasites & Vectors | **Year:** 2026 (19:54) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s13071-026-07259-7
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/vielma-2026-coluzzii-swarm-flight-speed-burkina-faso.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Three-dimensional photonic-fence videography of **swarming *Anopheles
coluzzii*** in semi-field conditions in Burkina Faso, at five operational sex
ratios (male-only, male-biased, balanced, female-biased, female-only), with
3 replicates per group. The study quantifies swarm volume, track density and
individual flight speed, and uses a Random Forest as an exploratory classifier
of sex ratio from kinematic signatures.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Mean flight speed of *A. coluzzii* (time-averaged per track, m/s):**
  male-only (1:0) **0.73 ± 0.02**, male-biased (3:1) **0.68 ± 0.05**,
  balanced (1:1) **0.66 ± 0.04**, female-biased (1:3) **0.79 ± 0.09**,
  female-only (0:1) **0.87 ± 0.05**.
- **Swarm volume** grows with the female fraction: **305.1 cm³** (male-biased)
  to **612.6 cm³** (female-only); swarm density and speed are negatively
  correlated (R²_spline = 0.712; GLMM R² = 0.581) and volume–speed relation
  is nonlinear (spline, R² = 0.504).
- The paper's own speed filter uses **0.50 m/s** as the lower bound for
  swarm-associated flight, citing previous swarm speeds of **0.50–0.59 m/s**.
- Energetic context: swarming flight consumes **> 50 % of available sugar and
  glycogen reserves** (cited from ref. 43).

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

The only source found that measures **flight speed of *A. coluzzii*** rather
than of a congenere or of *Aedes*. It supplies the two quantities the movement
law needs to convert a daily displacement budget into a physical speed: a
typical cruising speed of **0.66–0.87 m/s** and a lower bound of ~0.5 m/s for
coordinated flight. At 0.7 m/s a mosquito covers ~0.7 km in one hour of
sustained flight, which is the right order of magnitude for the observed
daily dispersal (0.4–0.7 km/day measured by MRR) — i.e. **daily dispersal is
consistent with roughly one hour of directed flight per day**, not with
continuous movement.
**Caveats:** the measurement is of **males in swarms**, at a swarm marker,
not of host-seeking or oviposition-seeking females; semi-field (mesh
enclosure), Burkina Faso, one site; the swarm speed is not a dispersal speed.
