# Videographic analysis of flight behaviours of host-seeking *Anopheles arabiensis* towards BG-Malaria trap

**Authors:** Emanuel P. A. Batista, S. A. Mapua, H. Ngowo, N. S. Matowo, E. F. Melo, K. S. Paixão, A. E. Eiras, F. O. Okumu
**Journal:** PLoS ONE | **Year:** 2019 (14(12):e0220563) | **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0220563
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/batista-2019-arabiensis-flight-bg-malaria-trap.md
**Copy read:** Europe PMC full text — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6668822

## Abstract (summary)

Videographic tracking of laboratory-reared female ***Anopheles arabiensis*** in
the vicinity of two mosquito traps (the BG-Malaria trap, BGM, and the
BG-Sentinel, BGS) to characterise the flight behaviour that determines capture
success. BGM was tested at three heights (20, 40, 80 cm) and with three bait
combinations (Ifakara blend + CO2, CO2 alone, no bait).

## Key Results (as used by the model)

### Sampling rate and apparatus  [M]
* **Three high-resolution infrared cameras recording at up to 68 frames per
  second** were used to track flight behaviour around the traps. This is the
  primary, species-relevant number for the Centinela's temporal-resolution
  question: the trajectories of host-seeking *Anopheles* around a strong
  attractant source were resolved at **≈ 68 Hz (≈ 15 ms per frame)**.
* Traps are cylindrical, **35 cm in diameter × 40 cm high**, each with an
  electrical fan (14 cm diameter, 12 V battery) producing the suction airflow
  that captures approaching mosquitoes.
* Mosquitoes: laboratory-reared *A. arabiensis* females; flight behaviour
  recorded in the trap's vicinity and classified by approach geometry.

### Behavioural findings  [P]
* **More mosquitoes were observed near the BGM than near the BGS.**
* **BGMs installed 20 cm above the floor and baited with CO2 received the most
  visits** by host-seeking mosquitoes, across the height and bait treatments.
* **Mosquitoes decelerate on approach to a strong odour/CO2 source**, adopting
  a sinuous flight pattern — the authors cite Cooperband & Cardé (1999) as
  consistent with this, and attribute part of the elevated observed velocity
  around the BGM to the trap's own flow field ("inverting the airflow in the
  BGM extends the area of high-speed flow more than 17 times").
* Absolute approach velocities are given per treatment in figure panels
  (cm s⁻¹) rather than as text values → **[NR] in text form**; the qualitative
  trend (deceleration + sinuosity near the source) is the usable content.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

* **Temporal-resolution precedent [M]:** a peer-reviewed *Anopheles* host-seeking
  videography used **68 fps** to resolve approach trajectories. Together with
  Hawkes & Gibson 2016 (50 Hz, *A. coluzzii*), Spitzen et al. 2013 (25 fps /
  0.04 s, *A. gambiae* s.s.), Cribellier et al. 2018 (90 fps, *A. coluzzii*) and
  Hinze et al. 2021 (60 fps, *A. gambiae* s.s.), the published empirical
  sampling band for malaria-vector host-seeking trajectories is
  **25–90 Hz** — i.e. **11–40 ms per frame**. A **1-second** integration step
  is 25–90× coarser than every one of these experiments.
* **Approach deceleration [P]:** the "terminal slowdown" of the Centinela
  movement law has an *Anopheles* proxy beyond *A. coluzzii* (Cribellier 2018
  and Hawkes & Gibson 2016 both measured it directly in *A. coluzzii*).
* **Trap-height / plume-height cue [P]:** the 20 cm installation height is
  consistent with the low-altitude host-seeking band measured by Hawkes &
  Gibson (dipping tracks below 3.5 cm; smooth/tortuous tracks at 11–14 cm).

**Caveats:** *A. arabiensis* (sister species of *A. coluzzii*) → behavioural
claims are **[P]**; the sampling rate is a property of the instrument and is
**[M]** for the technique. Laboratory release of fed/unfed females around an
artificial trap, not a natural host.
