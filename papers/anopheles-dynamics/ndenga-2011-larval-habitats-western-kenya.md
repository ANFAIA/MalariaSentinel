# A longitudinal study on Anopheles mosquito larval abundance in distinct geographical and environmental settings in western Kenya

**Authors:** Ndenga BA, Simbauni JA, Dombi JM, Kannu N, Githeko AK, Fillinger U
**Journal:** Malaria Journal | **Year:** 2011 | **DOI:** 10.1186/1475-2875-10-81
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/ndenga-2011-larval-habitats-western-kenya.md

---

## Key results (used by the Centinela)

- Weekly sampling of 20 permanent + 20 temporary habitats per site, three western Kenya sites (Fort Ternan, Lunyerere, Nyalenda), 2006-2007.
- Habitat types: drainage canals, rain pools, hoof prints, tire tracks, paddies, burrow pits; 86% of habitats were human-made.
- Anopheles arabiensis dominated in the highland sites (71% of An. gambiae s.l. in Fort Ternan).
- Early-instar larvae more abundant in temporary habitats (rain-pulse driven); late instars reduced in river fringes and cattle watering points vs rain pools.
- Larval abundance followed rainfall with a ~3-week lag (development + oviposition response).

## Complementary occupancy numbers (reviewed in Herrera-Varela 2015, LSHTM thesis DOI 10.17037/pubs.02222109)

- "Large-scale longitudinal studies in the floodplains of the Gambia River, rural lowland and highland areas in Kenya and in urban Dar es Salaam (Fillinger et al. 2008; Majambere et al. 2008; Ndenga et al. 2011) have shown that **on any given sampling date only 20-50% of aquatic habitats contained anopheline larvae**."
- Site-selection risk-factor analyses repeatedly fail to predict occupied habitats — occupancy is dynamic and heterogeneous.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Together with Bøgh 2004, this fixes the empirical range of Anopheles habitat occupancy used by **D1_habitat_occupancy** (band 0.20-0.70). Also a caution: occupancy varies strongly with season and habitat turnover — the scorer compares the final snapshot only, so single-snapshot occupancy is a noisy statistic (multi-seed/seasonal scoring recommended).
