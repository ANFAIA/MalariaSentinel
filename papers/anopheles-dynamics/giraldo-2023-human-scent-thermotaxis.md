# Human scent guides mosquito thermotaxis and host selection under naturalistic conditions

**Authors:** Diego Giraldo, Stephanie Rankin-Turner, Abel Corver, Genevieve M. Tauxe, Anne L. Gao, Dorian M. Jackson, Limonty Simubali, Christopher Book, et al.
**Journal:** Current Biology | **Year:** 2023 | **DOI:** 10.1016/j.cub.2023.04.050
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/giraldo-2023-human-scent-thermotaxis.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

The study shows that human scent guides mosquito thermotaxis and host
selection under naturalistic conditions: host odour and body heat act
together when mosquitoes choose where to land, and the response is strongest
during the host-seeking period of the night.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Host detection combines a volatile plume (CO2 and body odour) with a
  short-range thermal cue.
- This supports a finite host-seeking plume rather than a uniform, unlimited
  search (mechanism). The numeric scale and radius in
  `species.<row>.host_seeking_*` are engineering priors: the paper does not
  measure a metre-scale plume length.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Provenance for the mechanism of a finite host-seeking plume. The numeric
kernel parameters are documented as engineering priors, not as values measured
in this paper.
