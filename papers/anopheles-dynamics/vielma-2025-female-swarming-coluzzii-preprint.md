# The nocturnal swarming activity of the malaria mosquito: it's not just the males

**Authors:** Vielma S, Minuz R, Trusso A, Sawadogo S, Cribellier A, Diabate A, Simoni A, Muijres FT, Müller R
**Journal:** Preprint (Research Square) | **Year:** 2025 (posted 2025-10-01) | **DOI:** 10.21203/rs.3.rs-7045295/v1
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/vielma-2025-female-swarming-coluzzii-preprint.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Infrared 3-D tracking of **virgin female and male *Anopheles coluzzii*** under
laboratory conditions, during and after sunset, addressing the sex-specific
contribution to swarm structure and the persistence of female swarming after sunset.
**Preprint: not peer-reviewed; only the abstract was read.**

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Both sexes swarm.** Females **initiate swarming 14 s before males**.
- **Flight speed at the peak of swarming (at sunset): females flew 31 % faster than
  males — mean speeds of 0.67 m/s (females) and 0.51 m/s (males)**.
- Females showed a **broader dispersion** in spatial positioning.
- Females **maintain structured swarming activity for longer — up to at least one hour
  after sunset**.
- Both sexes formed aggregations **directly above the visual swarm-inducing ground
  marker**, suggesting **shared spatial orientation mechanisms**.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Evidence tier **[M]** for the coluzzii measurements (but **preprint, unverified** — no
peer review, no independent replication, and the project's rule forbids presenting
unverified claims as verified; treat every number here as **provisional**).

Three points of interest for the male layer:

1. **The male in-swarm speed is 0.51 m/s** here, versus **0.66–0.87 m/s** in
   `vielma-2026-coluzzii-swarm-flight-speed-burkina-faso.md` and **0.5 m/s** in
   `cribellier-2026-coluzzii-swarm-behavioural-rules-agent-based-model.md`. The
   difference is most plausibly a definition difference (time-averaged over complete
   looping tracks vs instantaneous straight-flight segments). **The Centinela must pick
   one convention and state it**, because the male daily-displacement budget (see
   `epopa-2017-male-coluzzii-mrr-bana-burkina-faso.md`) is converted into flight speed
   by the *same* number.
2. **Females swarm too**, and for **at least an hour after sunset** — i.e. the
   "swarm window" in the draft's D5 formula, if it is meant to be the only window in
   which mating can occur, is probably **too short**: a female that misses the
   27-minute male swarm peak may still encounter males later.
3. **Both sexes orient to the same marker**, which supports a **single marker field**
   serving both sexes rather than two.

**Caveats:** preprint; laboratory; the abstract does not report the female attendance
rate (how many females join a swarm), nor swarm size in the mixed-sex setting, nor
whether female swarming is at the same *sites* as male swarming; the 0.67/0.51 m/s
figures are means without dispersion in the abstract.
