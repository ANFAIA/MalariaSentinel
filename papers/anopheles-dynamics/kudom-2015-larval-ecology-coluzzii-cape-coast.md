# Larval ecology of Anopheles coluzzii in Cape Coast, Ghana: water quality, nature of habitat and implication for larval control

**Authors:** Arthur A. Kudom
**Journal:** Malaria Journal | **Year:** 2015 (14:447) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s12936-015-0989-4
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/kudom-2015-larval-ecology-coluzzii-cape-coast.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

15-month larval survey in Cape Coast (coastal savannah zone of Ghana, a
wetland-rich urban area without large commercial vegetable farming). Every
*Anopheles*-positive habitat (APL) was characterised for temperature, pH,
dissolved oxygen, salinity, conductivity, ammonium and bacterial fauna, and
compared with habitats colonized only by *Culex*. Sibling species were
identified by PCR.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- ***An. coluzzii* dominated in almost all APL habitats** found in Cape Coast,
  breeding in both rainy and dry seasons; *An. gambiae* and *An. melas* were
  also present. Rainy-season *An. coluzzii* habitats were typical *An. gambiae*
  habitats (footprints, tyre tracks, rain pools); in the dry season it
  coexisted with *Culex* in choked gutters and organically polluted habitats.
- **Salinity of APL habitats: median 0.8 ppt (IQR 0.4–2.3), N = 72**
  (pooled water 0.87 ppt, IQR 0.5–1.4; choked gutters 0.94, IQR 0.9–1.5);
  *Culex*-only habitats were not saltier (0.6 ppt, IQR 0.5–0.7).
- Other APL-habitat medians (IQR): temperature **34.9 °C (33.4–37.1)**,
  pH **8.1 (7.3–8.6)**, dissolved oxygen **7.6 mg/L (4.6–8.8)**,
  specific conductivity **1452.5 µS/cm (793–4209)**, TDS **952.9 mg/L
  (435–2371)**, ionised ammonia NH4+ **3.3 mg/L (2.4–4.2)**.
- **Oxygen is a hard filter:** ~48 % (22/46) of *Culex*-only habitats had no
  measurable dissolved oxygen, and ***An. coluzzii* was absent from such
  anoxic habitats**.
- Organic pollution: *An. coluzzii* tolerated faecal contamination
  (thermotolerant coliforms, *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Salmonella* present in
  every habitat) but its tolerance is **lower than *Culex*'s** (NH4+
  significantly higher in *Culex*-only habitats, p = 0.001).
- Conforms to the continental species-distribution model of Tene Fossog et al.
  (2015), which predicts *An. coluzzii* predominance along the West/Central
  African coastline; the author attributes Cape Coast dominance partly to
  the **high salinity of the local breeding habitats**.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

The closest *A. coluzzii*-specific field dataset on the **coastal Ghanaian
savannah zone** (same ecological zone as the AOI). It supplies the empirical
ranges for the aquatic part of the habitat contract: dissolved oxygen floor
(coluzzii absent in anoxic water), temperature range (33–37 °C), pH, salinity
(0.4–2.3 ppt IQR in the field), conductivity/TDS and ammonium thresholds that
separate *Anopheles* from *Culex* habitats. It also documents **year-round
breeding** (permanent + temporary habitats), which the "dry patch" semantics
of the engine must respect.
**Caveats:** Cape Coast habitats are freshwater-to-brackish urban water bodies
(salinity ≤ ~2.3 ppt IQR), *not* the mangrove/lagoon waters of the Accra
coastal AOI; single city, single year-plus; larval densities are not reported
per unit area in this paper.
