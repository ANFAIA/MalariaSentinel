# Frequency matters: How successive feeding episodes by blood-feeding insect vectors influences disease transmission

**Authors:** Douglas E. Brackney, Jacquelyn C. LaReau, Ryan C. Smith
**Journal:** PLoS Pathogens | **Year:** 2021 | **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.ppat.1009590
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/brackney-2021-frequency-matters-successive-feeding-episodes.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Review of how additional (successive) blood meals acquired by a vector within or across
gonotrophic cycles change pathogen transmission, across arboviruses, malaria parasites and
*Leishmania*. Its framing — "frequency matters" — is the epidemiological justification
for modelling the number of meals per cycle rather than assuming one.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- "mosquitoes that are responsible for transmitting human malaria parasites, are highly
  anthropophilic and will **frequently imbibe multiple blood meals within a single
  gonotrophic cycle** (the time from blood meal acquisition to egg laying; **approximately
  3 days**)." → confirms the ~3-day cycle against which multi-meal events must be packed.
- "*Ae. aegypti* acquire approximately **0.7 human blood meals a day** in nature." →
  a feeding-frequency reference value (Aedes).
- "blood meal analysis of sandflies revealed that **28 % of field-caught individuals
  contained remnants of blood from multiple host species**."
- **Malaria, transmission:** "Compared to a single blood meal, ***P. falciparum*-infected
  mosquitoes provided with an additional blood meal increase oocyst growth rates and
  sporogony, resulting in faster rates of sporozoite salivary gland invasion and reducing
  the time required for malaria parasite transmission**."
- "When an additional blood meal is provided to *Plasmodium*-infected *Anopheles gambiae*
  **4 days post-infection** to mimic the natural feeding cycle, … oocysts of the rodent
  malaria parasite, *P. berghei*, were significantly limited by an additional feeding,
  while **no effect on the human malaria parasite, *P. falciparum*, was determined**."
- "modelling these changes in virus dissemination kinetics revealed that the shortened
  extrinsic incubation period (EIP) associated with additional blood meals could
  significantly increase the basic reproductive number, *R0*".
- "**vertical transmission** will only occur if an infected individual with a disseminated
  infection imbibes additional blood meals."

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Two consequences for the redesign, both concrete: (1) an extra meal can **shorten the
effective EIP** (faster sporogony / earlier salivary-gland invasion), so the second meal
of a cycle is worth more than a plain bite; (2) the *P. berghei* vs *P. falciparum*
asymmetry is a warning that the effect of an extra meal on parasite establishment is
species-specific and should not be assumed to help the parasite in the malaria case.

Caveats: a review of the *arbovirus* literature with a malaria section; the *Ae. aegypti*
0.7 meals/day figure does not transfer to *Anopheles*; the malaria numbers come from
rodent-parasite experiments plus *P. falciparum* laboratory work.
