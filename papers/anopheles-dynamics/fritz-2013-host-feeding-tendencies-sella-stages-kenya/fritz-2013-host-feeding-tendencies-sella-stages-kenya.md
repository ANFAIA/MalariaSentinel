# Host-feeding tendencies of *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. in western Kenya: blood-meal identification by Sella stage and PCR

**Authors:** M. L. Fritz, J. R. Miller, M. N. Bayoh, J. M. Vulule, J. R. Landgraf, E. D. Walker
**Journal:** Medical and Veterinary Entomology | **Year:** 2013 | **Volume/Pages:** 27(4): 398–407 | **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1365-2915.2012.01063.x
**Title (as published):** "Application of a reverse dot blot DNA-DNA hydridization method to quantify host-feeding tendencies of two sibling species in the *Anopheles gambiae* complex"
**Open access URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3818715/

## Abstract (summary)

Field collection of blood-fed *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. in Kisian, western Kenya
(June–July 2008), indoors (walls of human dwellings) and outdoors (clay pots),
with the abdominal condition of every female recorded on the **Sella scale**
before blood-meal identification by host-PCR. Used here for two things: the
**operational range of Sella stages collected in the field** (2–6, i.e. from
freshly-fed to sub-gravid) and the **relation between abdominal/blood state and
host choice**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Collection protocol and state range: "Blood fed mosquitoes, ranging from
  **freshly-fed to sub-gravid (Sella stages 2-6)** were collected indoors from
  the walls of human dwellings, and outdoors from clay pots"; "In total, **299
  *An. gambiae* s.l. mosquitoes were collected with either fresh blood or a
  range of Sella stages (from 2 to 6), including subgravids**."
  → in a tropical setting, field females are caught across the whole
  blood-digestion range, so a model must represent the **fed-but-not-yet-gravid
  (sub-gravid / semi-gravid)** state, not just "fed" vs "gravid".
- Species composition of the blood-fed catch: "*An. arabiensis* comprised
  **69.5%** of the mosquitoes collected and identified, while **29.5%** were
  *An. gambiae*"; indoor vs outdoor: *An. gambiae* s.s. 23.9 % of the catch
  (indoor 19.3 %), *An. arabiensis* 57.8 % (indoor 22.4 %).
- Host identity vs resting site / blood state: "Only 3 host animals were found
  to have been fed upon by *An. gambiae* and *An. arabiensis*: **humans, cattle,
  and birds**. **Ninety-eight percent of the *An. arabiensis* fed upon cattle**,
  and more than 60% of these individuals were collected from indoor resting
  sites. … Only 2 *An. gambiae* were collected from outdoor resting sites, and
  both fed upon bovine hosts. Of the 13 *An. gambiae* collected from indoor
  resting sites, 6 contained bovine blood meals and 7 contained human blood
  meals." Discussion: "even indoor collections were dominated by individuals
  that had fed on cattle blood, thus **indoor resting behavior does not
  necessarily correlate with human host selection**".
- Method note: PCR host identification worked "in field-collected mosquitoes in
  varying stages of blood digestion" — i.e. **the abdominal state does not
  destroy host DNA**, so a blood-state-stratified host analysis is feasible in
  field data.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

- Gives the field **prevalence structure** that a `blood_state` variable must
  represent: females are caught across Sella 2–6, so "fed" is a *continuum* of
  digestion stages (freshly-fed → sub-gravid → gravid), each with a different
  probability of still being responsive to host cues.
- Gives the **host-choice caveat**: blood state and resting site are
  confounders for host preference. Any anthropophily term in the model must not
  be applied uniformly across blood states, because host identity in field
  collections varies strongly with the abdominal condition (cattle-dominated in
  outdoor/blood-fed collections).
- Caveats: western Kenya, 2008, *An. gambiae* s.l. (no *coluzzii*); the host
  frequencies are for a bed-net-protected rural village; blood state was scored
  externally, not by ovarian dissection.
