# Effects of natal habitat odour, reinforced by adult experience, on choice of oviposition site in the mosquito *Aedes aegypti*

**Authors:** CE Hamilton, DV Beresford, JF Sutcliffe
**Journal:** Medical and Veterinary Entomology | **Year:** 2011 (25:129–137) | **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1365-2915.2011.00964.x
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/hamilton-2011-aedes-natal-habitat-preference-induction.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Laboratory test of **natal habitat preference induction (NHPI)** in ***Aedes
aegypti***. Females were exposed to a dilute repellent (the "inducing stimulus") in
their **breeding water** (aquatic stages) and/or in the **air** (adult stage) under
six life-stage exposure regimes — L (larval only), LP (larval + pupal), LPE (+
emergent adult), LPEA (+adult), PEA (pupal + emergent adult + adult) and A (adult
only). Control mosquitoes were reared identically but without the stimulus. Gravid
females were then given a **two-choice oviposition assay**: water with the inducing
stimulus vs water without.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Sample size: "Of the 435 mosquitoes tested in the experiment, 176 were
  non-distributors (i.e. laid all of their eggs in only one of the choices)."**
  So **~40 % of females were non-distributors** and put all eggs in a single choice —
  i.e. **site fidelity at the scale of a two-choice arena is the minority behaviour**
  in this species too.
- **The effect requires exposure across the whole life:** "Treatment females
  (distributors plus non-distributors) reared in the presence of the inducing stimulus
  **throughout their lives (LPEA regime)** showed a significant preference for the
  oviposition option containing the inducing stimulus (**24/36 females**) compared with
  corresponding controls (**5/39 females**)."
- **Adult exposure alone is required for the effect to be expressed:** "Distributors
  reared under the LPEA and PEA regimes also showed this preference (**6/6** treatment vs
  **2/29** control females, and **13/18** treatment vs **7/23** control females,
  respectively). Females that had been exposed to the inducing stimulus as **either
  immatures or adults only** showed **no preference** for, and some showed an
  **aversion** to, the treatment oviposition option."
- **Author's interpretation:** "This is interpreted as evidence for a **natal habitat
  preference induction (NHPI)** in this species, albeit one that **requires extensive
  reinforcement in the adult stage**."

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Evidence tier **[P]** — *Aedes aegypti*, a different genus (container breeder, urban,
dengue vector), not *Anopheles*. Included here as the **methodological reference and
the canonical definition of the phenomenon** the draft's `home_x/home_y` field would
be invoking.

What it establishes for the Centinela's argument:

- **"Natal habitat preference induction" is an olfactory-learning phenomenon, not a
  navigational one.** The mosquito learns an *odour*, and the odours only elicit the
  preference if **re-exposed in adulthood**. A coordinate-based `home_x/home_y` is a
  completely different mechanism (spatial memory / path integration) and the NHPI
  literature does not license it. If the Centinela wanted to model natal-site
  attraction, NHPI would be the *cue-based* way to do it.
- **Even in the species where NHPI is strongest, the majority of females do not
  concentrate** (176/435 non-distributors; and in the key LPEA comparison only
  **24/36 = 67 %** preferred the natal-stimulus option against a control of
  **5/39 = 13 %**). A model that assumes a high return rate would be wrong even for
  *Aedes*.
- **The effect was "no preference" or even "aversion" without lifelong + adult
  reinforcement** — a fragility that argues strongly against hard-coding natal
  attraction into a vector model without measured parameters.

**Caveats:** *Aedes aegypti*, laboratory colonies, a single dilute repellent as the
inducing stimulus; the "natal habitat" is a **chemical cue in a cup**, not a place;
no distances or coordinates are involved anywhere; the full text was not read (only
the abstract) — the L/ LP/ A-only group results beyond the quoted sentences were not
verified.
