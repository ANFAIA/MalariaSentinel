# Comparison of entomological impacts of two methods of intervention designed to control *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. via swarm killing in Western Burkina Faso

**Authors:** Sawadogo SP, Niang A, Wu SL, Millogo AA, Bonds J, Latham M, Dabiré RK, Tatarsky A, Tripet F, Diabaté A
**Journal:** Scientific Reports | **Year:** 2022 (12:12397) | **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-022-16649-7
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/sawadogo-2022-swarm-killing-intervention-burkina-faso.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

The **first large-scale randomised field test of swarm killing** as a malaria vector
control intervention: in 10 villages of western Burkina Faso, **targeted (focalised)
space spraying of swarm sites** was compared with **broadcast space spraying**, each
evaluated against a control village, on entomological endpoints (human biting rate,
insemination rate, parity rate, male and female resting density, EIR). The paper
states the rationale that makes the male layer epidemiologically real: the swarming
behaviour of male and female *Anopheles* is an **outdoor, nocturnal activity that LLINs
and IRS cannot reach**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Swarm-site targeting is spatially cheap:** the number of swarms was **not**
  correlated with village size (R² = 0.15, P = 0.12) but **was** correlated with
  **marker abundance**; the quantity of insecticide used in **targeted** spraying was
  **not** correlated with village size (R² = 0.08, P = 0.80), whereas with broadcast
  spraying it was (R² > 0.98, P < 0.05). Targeted spraying scales with **swarm
  abundance**, not with village area.
- **Both interventions reduced insemination rate and parity rate significantly
  (P < 0.001)**; GEE models gave very similar results to GLM.
- **Both interventions reduced female density** (targeted P = 0.003, broadcast
  P = 0.001) and male density significantly; pre-intervention density was a highly
  significant negative predictor in both cases (P < 0.001).
- **EIR:** the EIR increased in the control villages after the intervention and showed
  a **trend of reduction in the villages treated with targeted spraying**; broadcast
  spraying gave very low EIR values with no variation between intervention periods.
- **Prior evidence quoted:** in a preliminary single-village experiment, mass killing of
  swarming mosquitoes produced a **~80 % decrease in female indoor densities**, and the
  proportion of **younger males rose from 20 % to 70 %** (i.e. the intervention
  preferentially removed **old** males).
- **Swarm-site persistence across species and countries:** "males systematically use the
  **same sites to swarm over time and even after several years**"; this behaviour is
  reported for Mali, Burkina Faso, Benin, Sudan, Cameroon, The Gambia, São Tomé and
  Príncipe, Mozambique and Tanzania.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Evidence tier **[P]** (field, *A. gambiae* s.l., western Burkina Faso — the same
Sudanian-Sahelian rice-growing setting as the Ghanaian pilot).

This paper is the **intervention-side justification for having a male layer at all**, and
it also constrains how the layer must be built:

1. **The epidemiological channel is demonstrated in the field**: killing males at swarm
   sites **reduces female insemination rate** (P < 0.001) and **reduces female
   density**. This is the fertilisation-limitation channel that Zhu et al. 2017 could
   not represent, and it is the strongest published argument that a male module should
   be able to transmit a male-mortality shock to female reproduction.
2. **Swarm targeting is a per-marker operation, not a per-area one.** A model whose
   male layer cannot distinguish "males at a marker" from "males spread over the
   village" cannot represent this intervention: with the aggregate, the intervention
   removes males **only at swarms**, and the females that are not near a swarm are
   unaffected. This argues for the marker field being an explicit, spatially discrete
   object even in the aggregate option.
3. **Swarm-site persistence over years** means the marker layer is a **static
   geographic feature**, and the "swarm marker density" free number in the draft should
   be a **per-marker, persistent** quantity, not a nightly draw.
4. The ~**80 % reduction in female indoor density** after swarm killing (preliminary
   experiment) is a **field-anchored prior for the strength of the male→female
   channel**, usable to sanity-check the D5 formula's response to a male-kill shock.

**Caveats:** the intervention effect is not a clean "mating limitation only" signal —
it is confounded with additional male mortality from space spraying at dusk and with
general insecticide effects; EIR results are secondary and underpowered; *A. gambiae*
s.l., not *coluzzii*; the ~80 % figure comes from an earlier, single-village
experiment cited by this paper, not from the 2022 trial itself.
