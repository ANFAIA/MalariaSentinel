# The use of sequential mark-release-recapture experiments to estimate population size, survival and dispersal of male mosquitoes of the Anopheles gambiae complex in Bana, Burkina Faso

**Authors:** Epopa PA, Millogo AA, Collins CM, North A, Tripet F, Benedict MQ, Diabate A
**Journal:** Parasites & Vectors | **Year:** 2017 (10:376) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s13071-017-2310-6
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/epopa-2017-male-coluzzii-mrr-bana-burkina-faso.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Four sequential mark-release-recapture (MRR) experiments on **male *Anopheles
coluzzii*** in Bana village (West African Sudan-savana, Burkina Faso), during
the wet and dry seasons of 2013–2015. ~5,000 (3,407–5,273) field-collected
adult males were marked with three dye colours and released at three
locations (village centre, village edge, outside the village); recaptures over
the following 7 days by clay pots, pyrethroid spray catch and swarm sampling.
Population size and daily survival were estimated with a Bayesian version of
the Fisher–Ford model.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Net dispersal (straight-line release→recapture) of *A. coluzzii* males:
  from 40 m to 549 m**, with no seasonal effect (F(3,192) = 0.13, P > 0.05)
  and no effect of capture method (F(2,195) = 0.18, P > 0.05).
- **Release location explains > 44 % of the variance** in net dispersal
  (F(2,197) = 83.52, P < 0.001): mosquitoes released outside the village
  dispersed furthest, those released at the edge next, those released at the
  centre least — and recaptures concentrated in the village core, "which also
  has a higher density of human population", lending support to the
  hypothesis that *Anopheles* are attracted to human populations.
- **Day effect:** mosquitoes recaptured on day 1 were significantly closer to
  the release point than on any later day (F(1,197) = 12.77, P < 0.001); all
  later days were indistinguishable.
- **Daily survival (swarm + spray-catch combined):** **0.700 ± 0.068
  (Oct 2013), 0.835 ± 0.041 (May 2014), 0.865 ± 0.029 (Sep 2014),
  0.692 ± 0.064 (Apr 2015)**; all experiments pooled **0.773 ± 0.094**.
  Swarm sampling alone gives consistently higher survival than spray catch.
- **Population size:** ~10,000–50,000 males at the end of the dry season vs
  ~100,000–500,000 in the wet season (≈10-fold increase); survival showed no
  identifiable seasonal pattern.
- The authors note that net straight-line distance is a **lower bound on
  flight range**, since a few individuals dispersed > 500 m.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

The **only *A. coluzzii*-specific MRR dispersal and survival measurement from
mainland West Africa** and therefore the anchor for the *coluzzii* row of the
movement law. It gives (i) a local-scale dispersal range of 40–549 m over
7 days for males, (ii) a **destination bias toward the human settlement core**
that supports a *directed* component in the movement kernel, and (iii) a
*coluzzii* daily survival of ~0.70–0.87.
**Caveats:** **males**, not blood-feeding females (the transmission-relevant
stage); a single Sahelian-Sudanian village, not the coastal AOI; daily survival
from marked (handling-dye) individuals is likely conservative; the study
provides no distribution *shape* (exponential vs half-Cauchy) for the
dispersal kernel.
