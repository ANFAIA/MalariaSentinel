# Signatures of aestivation and migration in Sahelian malaria mosquito populations

**Authors:** Adama Dao, Alpha Seydou Yaro, Moussa Diallo, Seydou Timbiné, Diana L. Huestis, Yaya Kassogué, Adama I. Traoré, Zana L. Sanogo, Djibril Samaké, Tovi Lehmann
**Journal:** Nature | **Year:** 2014 | **Volume/Pages:** 516(7531): 387–390 | **DOI:** 10.1038/nature13987 | **PMID:** 25470038
**Open access URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4306333/ (NIHMS author manuscript, NIHMS637451)

## Abstract (summary)

Five years of continuous entomological sampling (September 2008 – August 2013) in
the Sahelian village of **Thierola, Mali** (511 collection days, 40,195
*An. gambiae* s.l. of which 28,547 females and 11,648 males) with time-series
(unobserved-components) analysis of the seasonal component of the density of
*A. coluzzii*, *A. gambiae* s.s. and *A. arabiensis*. Provides the **species-level
signature that separates local dry-season persistence (aestivation) from
recolonisation by long-distance migration (LDM)**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- The question and the species split: "how these vectors persist over the
  **3–6 month-long dry season** has not been resolved"; "**the population growth
  phase of both *A. gambiae* and *A. arabiensis* lagged by two months. Such a
  delay is incompatible with local persistence, but fits LDM**. Surviving the long
  dry season **in situ allows *A. coluzzii*** to predominate and become the primary
  force of malaria transmission."
- Species identity: "*A. coluzzii* (previously known as the *A. gambiae* S and M
  molecular forms)" — relevant because much of the older "M-form" literature
  (Yaro 2012, Lehmann 2010) is a mixture of *coluzzii* and *gambiae* s.s.
- Seasonal structure of *A. coluzzii*: "The early wet-season decline of
  *A. coluzzii* produced the **pre-dry-season trough in mid-November, before the
  last larval site dried**, which was followed by an **early dry-season peak in
  late December**." "Based on their 95% CIs, **one wet-season peak and two
  dry-season peaks, which were observed in all years**, were statistically
  supported." The seasonal component was highly significant (P < 0.0001) and
  **fixed** (not time-varying).
- *A. gambiae* s.s. in contrast: "**Only a single wet-season peak and a long
  dry-season trough** were statistically discerned in *A. gambiae* s.s."; "First of
  all, *A. coluzzii* was **present throughout the dry season (albeit in small
  numbers), whereas *A. gambiae* s.s. was undetected from January to May**";
  "Secondly, the density of *A. coluzzii* **rose dramatically (ten- to ninety-fold
  from their preceding phase) twice during the dry season**"; "Since these peaks
  **preceded the first rain by at least six weeks**, any potential migrant
  mosquitoes would likely perish before reproductive opportunities were
  available … (ruling out dry-season reproduction)"; "This phase started in
  **June for *A. coluzzii*** but in **August for *A. gambiae* s.s. and *A.
  arabiensis***" (a "delay of six to eight weeks").
- Composition: "*A. coluzzii* comprised **75% of the overall indoor vector
  density** with its wet-season peak being at least twice as high and broader than
  that of either *A. gambiae* s.s. or *A. arabiensis*."
- Interpretive caveat the authors stress (relevant to the model's dry-season
  rules): "**the most complex dynamics occurred during the dry season**, when the
  density of *A. coluzzii* fluctuated dramatically, **peaking when migration would
  seem highly unlikely**, while *A. gambiae* s.s. was undetected."

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

- **Species-differentiated dry-season semantics**: for the pilot species
  *A. coluzzii*, the dry season is a period of **in-situ persistence** (females
  present through the dry season, with a measurable dry-season density peak in
  late December), not a collapse; for *A. gambiae* s.s. the dry-season density
  trough is long and the population is re-established by migration. The Centinela
  has one species per run, so the *coluzzii* row should carry a **dry-season
  survival/persistence branch** (aestivation) that the *gambiae* row should not.
- **Provenance for the "dry patch is not lethal for the adult" semantics**:
  *coluzzii* density in Mali does not go to zero when the larval sites dry
  (November); it troughs in mid-November and peaks again in late December —
  i.e. surviving adults, not a die-off.
- Caveats: single village in Mali (Thierola), *An. gambiae* s.l. pooled for the
  overall analysis and separated by PCR for the time-series; density (not
  physiology) is the measured quantity; the seasonal component is fitted from
  5 years of data, so it encodes local hydro-climatology, not a universal rule.
