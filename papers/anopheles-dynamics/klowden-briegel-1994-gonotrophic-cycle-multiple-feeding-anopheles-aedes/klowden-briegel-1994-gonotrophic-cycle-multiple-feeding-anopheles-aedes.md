# Mosquito gonotrophic cycle and multiple feeding potential: contrasts between *Anopheles* and *Aedes* (Diptera: Culicidae)

**Authors:** Michael J. Klowden, Hans Briegel
**Journal:** Journal of Medical Entomology | **Year:** 1994 | **DOI:** 10.1093/jmedent/31.4.618
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/klowden-briegel-1994-gonotrophic-cycle-multiple-feeding-anopheles-aedes.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Five mosquito species (two aedine, three anopheline) were allowed to blood-feed **to
repletion** and their host-seeking behaviour was then measured with an olfactometer at
intervals. The central result is a genus-level contrast: *Aedes* females stop seeking a
host once engorged, whereas *Anopheles* females keep seeking hosts **while their eggs
mature**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- "Both *Aedes aegypti* (L.) and *Ae. albopictus* (Skuse) were inhibited from subsequently
  seeking a host during the gonotrophic cycle, but ***Anopheles gambiae* Giles, *An.
  albimanus* Wiedemann, and *An. freeborni* Aitken, all showed varying degrees of
  host-seeking behavior while their eggs matured**."
- "Continued host-seeking by some anophelines during the gonotrophic cycle has important
  implications for some assumptions commonly made about mosquito behavior and parasite
  transmission."
- Experimental design note: the females were fed **to repletion** first, so the continued
  seeking observed is *after* a complete meal — i.e. it is not merely a consequence of a
  partial meal.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This is the primary experimental justification for the redesign: in *Anopheles* the
"one meal ⇒ enter BLOOD_FED ⇒ stop seeking" transition is wrong in two ways — (i) the
meal may be partial (see `briegel-horler-1993-…`, `klowden-lea-1978-…`), and (ii) even
after a full meal the female may continue to seek hosts during egg maturation, which is a
direct transmission multiplier (more bites per gonotrophic cycle ⇒ more chances to acquire
gametocytes and to infect a human).

Caveats: laboratory olfactometer, 1994, *An. gambiae* (Giles) of an unspecified strain;
no Ghana population; the abstract reports "varying degrees" without a numeric fraction,
so it cannot be used to fix a probability, only to justify the mechanism.
