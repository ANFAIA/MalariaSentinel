# Peculiar liquid-feeding and pathogen transmission behavior of *Aedes togoi* and comparison with *Anopheles sinensis*

**Authors:** Seung-Jae Lee, Donghyun Kang, Seung-Chan Lee, Yu-Ren Ha
**Journal:** Scientific Reports | **Year:** 2016 | **DOI:** 10.1038/srep20464
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/lee-2016-liquid-feeding-aedes-togoi-anopheles-sinensis.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Micro-particle image velocimetry of the liquid-feeding process inside the proboscis of
*Aedes togoi* and *Anopheles sinensis*, to quantify flow rate, pumping frequency, stroke
volume and wall shear stress, and to relate them to pathogen transmission.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- "The average flow rate of *Ae. togoi* during the intake stage is **27.3 ± 17.1 nl/s**, and
  that of the ejection stage is **5.6 ± 2.4 nl/s**."
- "the average flow rate of *An. sinensis* is **12.7 ± 1.7 nl/s** and **0.7 ± 0.7 nl/s** in
  the intake and ejection stages, respectively."
- "The **stroke volume** was obtained by dividing the average flow rate by the pumping
  frequency" — i.e. the per-sip volume is a *derived* quantity, not a directly measured
  one.
- "The flow rates and ratio of the ejection volume of *Aedes togoi* were markedly higher
  than those of *Anophels sinensis*."
- "the proportion of the ejection stage to the feeding period are also different
  (**15.3 ± 6.7 %** and **5.6 ± 5.1 %** for *Ae. togoi* and *An. sinensis*)".
- Proboscis diameter and wall shear stress differ strongly between the two species.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Supplies the *flow-rate* scale that converts an ingestion duration into a volume:
`V_total ≈ intake flow rate × ingestion duration`. Combining the *Anopheles* intake rate
(12.7 nl/s) with the *coluzzii* ingestion time measured in `konopka-2026-…` (128 s) gives
an order-of-magnitude of ≈1.6 µL per meal, i.e. the same order as the 3 µL review value
and the 3–4 µL *Anopheles* estimate — but the two published estimates of the *Anopheles*
intake rate disagree in sign (here *Aedes* is faster; in `konopka-2026-…` *Anopheles* is
said to take the larger meal because of per-sip volume), and this extract deliberately
does **not** hide the inconsistency.

Caveats: *An. sinensis* (Asian proxy) and *Ae. togoi*; measurements were made on liquid
feed through the proboscis (artificial feeding), not on a live host, so they are
mechanistic constants, not field blood-meal sizes.
