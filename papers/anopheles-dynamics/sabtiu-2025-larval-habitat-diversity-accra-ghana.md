# Larval habitat diversity, physicochemical characteristics and their effect on the larval density of malaria vectors in the city of Accra, Ghana

**Authors:** Abubakar R. M. Sabtiu, Isaac A. Hinne, Ibrahim K. Sraku, David K. Halou, Robert T. Doe, Samuel K. Attah, Francis Aboagye-Antwi, Yaw A. Afrane
**Journal:** Malaria Journal | **Year:** 2025 (24:299) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s12936-025-05540-1
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/sabtiu-2025-larval-habitat-diversity-accra-ghana.md

## Abstract (summary)

Larval surveys were carried out at 15 locations in Accra (Greater Accra, coastal
savannah zone, Ghana) during the dry (Feb–Mar) and rainy (Jun–Jul) seasons of
2022, using the WHO standard dipping method. Sites were stratified into five
categories: irrigated urban farming (IUF), low / middle / high socio-economic
status (LS / MS / HS) and peri-urban (PU). Physicochemical parameters were
measured in every habitat and *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. were identified
morphologically and then by PCR (species-specific PCR plus PCR-RFLP for
*An. gambiae* s.s. vs *An. coluzzii*, ITS sequencing for *An. stephensi*).
727 potential breeding habitats were found; 475 (65.34 %) contained *Anopheles*
larvae. Drainage ditches were the most common positive habitat (229/475,
48.21 %). *An. coluzzii* was the predominant malaria vector. This is the only
study located that measures larval habitat physicochemistry and species
composition **inside the AOI city of the reference AOI (`accra-coastal-test`)**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Species composition of *An. gambiae* s.l. (679 typed): *An. coluzzii*
  **54.79 % (372/677)** in the Results text (the Abstract of the same paper
  reports **54.4 % (368/677)** — the paper is internally inconsistent, both
  numbers are quoted here), *An. gambiae* s.s. **42.97 % (285/679)**,
  *An. gambiae*/*An. coluzzii* hybrids **2.81 % (19/679)**, *An. stephensi*
  **0.44 % (3/679)** — first record of *An. stephensi* in Ghana.
- *An. coluzzii* dominated in every site category except HS and PU, where
  *An. gambiae* s.s. dominated (HS: s.s. 59 / coluzzii 50; PU: s.s. 111 /
  coluzzii 30). Supports `species.default_species: coluzzii` for the Accra
  coastal AOI but with a peri-urban caveat.
- Larval abundance: *An. gambiae* s.l. 22,919 individuals; highest in
  irrigated urban farming sites — **27.24 % (6244/22,919)** of the total, and
  **77.01 % (17,650/22,919)** of all larvae were collected in the rainy season.
  Larval density per dip reaches **5.02 larvae/dip** (drainage ditch, IUF,
  rainy season) and **4.75** (footprint, IUF, dry season); most habitats are
  in the 0.2–4 larvae/dip range.
- Physicochemistry of polluted (household-effluent) vs unpolluted habitats:
  ammonium **3.4 vs 1.3 mg/L NH4–N**, dissolved oxygen **34 % vs 52.9 %**,
  conductivity **5106 vs 2049 µS/cm**, TDS **3181 vs 1255 mg/L**. Salinity in
  the Accra habitats of the four sampled neighbourhoods was low:
  **0.6–4.4 ppt** (e.g. Nima 3.9 ppt polluted / 1.4 unpolluted; Teshie
  4.4 / 0.9; Madina 0.8 / 0.6).
- 12 habitat types identified (drainage ditch, tyre track, furrow, footprint,
  hoof print, puddle, swamp, pit, well, artificial pond, natural pond, car
  tyre), with breeding persisting year-round ("breeding sites persisted
  year-round, supporting continuous malaria transmission").

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Primary local evidence for (i) the *A. coluzzii* dominance in the reference
AOI city, (ii) the relative weight of Accra habitat types (drainage ditch ≫
tyre track > swamp > footprint/tyre-track rain pools), and (iii) the water
chemistry that the habitat-availability contract has to reproduce (DO,
conductivity, ammonium). **Caveats:** the salinity values measured here
(≤4.4 ppt) are from shallow, rain-fed urban habitats, not from the brackish
mangrove/lagoon habitats that define the coastal AOI; larval densities are
per-dip counts, not per-surface-area densities; the study is a single city and
two seasons.
