# The practical importance of permanent and semipermanent habitats for controlling aquatic stages of Anopheles gambiae sensu lato mosquitoes: operational observations from a rural town in western Kenya

**Authors:** Christine Bøgh, Ulrike Fillinger, Stephen Munga, Elijah Oyieke, Willem Takken, Bart GJ Knols, Wei Zheng, Bart Knols
**Journal:** Tropical Medicine & International Health | **Year:** 2004 | **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1365-3156.2004.01335.x
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/bogh-2004-larval-habitats-mbita.md

---

## Key results (used by the Centinela)

- 20-month weekly larval survey in Mbita (Lake Victoria, western Kenya), perennial transmission setting.
- **On average 67% of all available aquatic habitats on a given sampling date were colonized by Anopheles larvae** (83% Oct 2000-Mar 2001; 60% after Mar 2001); 89% contained mosquito larvae of any genus.
- All identified Anopheles were An. gambiae s.l.; natural and artificial habitats equally productive; habitat permanence had no significant influence on productivity so long as water was present.
- Late instars = only 25% of dipped larvae → high larval mortality.
- Anopheles found at varying densities in habitats of ALL stability levels.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Anchors scorer **D1_habitat_occupancy** (mal_core/scoring/scorers/d1_habitat_occupancy.py): the fraction of habitat cells occupied by the simulated population should fall in the 0.20-0.70 band observed by longitudinal field studies (Bøgh 67% avg; Ndenga 2011 24-41%; Fillinger/Majambere 20-50%). Occupancy is a season- and site-dependent quantity — the band is deliberately wide; occupancy ≈ 0 means extinction, ≈ 1 means every habitat cell saturated (unrealistic given larval mortality and oviposition selectivity).
