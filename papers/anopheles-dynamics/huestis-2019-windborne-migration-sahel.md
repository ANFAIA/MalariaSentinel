# Windborne long-distance migration of malaria mosquitoes in the Sahel

**Authors:** Diana L. Huestis, Adama Dao, Moussa Diallo, Zana L. Sanogo, Djibril Samake, Alpha S. Yaro, Yossi Ousman, Yvonne-Marie Linton, et al.
**Journal:** Nature | **Year:** 2019 | **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-019-1622-4
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/huestis-2019-windborne-migration-sahel.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Aerial sampling in the Sahel captured large numbers of malaria mosquitoes at
altitude, including blood-fed females, showing that *Anopheles* can be carried
long distances by the wind and that this migration connects the dry-season
population to distant larval habitats.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Windborne migration is a real dispersal pathway for Sahelian *Anopheles*.
- The engine represents it as a wind-advection term with a flight duration and
  a survival probability, keyed by `dispersal.wind_flight_hours` and
  `dispersal.wind_survival`.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Provenance for modelling wind-assisted migration as a distinct dispersal
channel from local Gaussian dispersal.
