# Estimates of the population size and dispersal range of *Anopheles arabiensis* in Northern KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa: implications for a planned pilot programme to release sterile male mosquitoes

**Authors:** Kaiser ML, Wood OR, Damiens D, Brooke BD, Koekemoer LL, Munhenga G
**Journal:** Parasites & Vectors | **Year:** 2021 (14:205) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s13071-021-04674-w
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/kaiser-2021-arabiensis-male-mrr-sit-south-africa.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

A **male-only mark-release-recapture study** of *Anopheles arabiensis* in northern
KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, run to inform a **sterile insect technique (SIT)**
programme: marked males were released with cloth tubes and clay pots, and recaptured
over 7 days; the paper estimates male population size, daily survival and **dispersal
range**, and reports the males' **swarm** behaviour.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Recapture rates were very low:** **0.09 %, 0.11 %, 0.06 % and 0.04 %** of released
  marked males were recaptured in the four experiments (MRR PT, MRRI at site C, MRRI at
  site E, MRRII) — a marked-population signal comparable to the low rates reported for
  *coluzzii* in Burkina Faso.
- **Male daily survival / life expectancy:** average life expectancy of **2.94 days
  (MRR PT), 2.59 days (MRRI C), 4.48 days (MRRI E) and 2.59 days (MRRII)**, using
  cloth-tube catches.
- **Male population size:** **539, 783, 6 213 and 401 males per hectare** (survival-
  adjusted index), or 2 819, 1 936, 9 643 and 2 193 males/ha with the simple index.
- **Male dispersal:** in the pilot trial (MRR PT) **no marked male was recaptured beyond
  100 m** from the release point; marked males were found only in the cloth tubes at
  **50 m (2 males) and 100 m (5 males)**, giving an **average dispersal distance of
  85.7 m**. Marked males were detected **only until the 4th day** of 7 days of
  collections.
- **Swarms:** released males **formed their own swarms on the day of release, near the
  release location**; in MRRI, **55.55 % (5 of 9) swarms found contained marked
  males**; **one marked male was recaptured 8 days after release while checking for
  swarms** during routine surveillance — i.e. a male still present at a swarm site
  **8 days** after release.
- Marked males were also recovered **by swarm sampling**, which the authors note was
  the method giving the highest recapture of males.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Evidence tier **[P]** (*A. arabiensis*, South Africa — a close relative of *coluzzii*,
but a different vector with different ecology; **not** mainland West Africa).

This extract is the **second male-only MRR dataset for the *gambiae* complex** and it
matters for three calibration decisions:

1. **Male dispersal is short and local.** 85.7 m average, nothing beyond 100 m in the
   pilot, versus **40–549 m over 7 days** for *coluzzii* in Burkina Faso
   (`epopa-2017-male-coluzzii-mrr-bana-burkina-faso.md`). The two are not
   contradictory (different regions, methods and climates) but they bracket the
   plausible values: the male movement kernel used by the Centinela should be
   **no broader than the coluzzii MRR figure**, and the male's daily displacement
   should be treated as **substantially smaller than the female's MDTpD of
   486–695 m/day** (`wickramasooriya-2026-coluzzii-mrr-saotome-principe.md`).
2. **Male life expectancy is short** (2.6–4.5 days in this South African setting,
   i.e. daily survival well below the 0.87 used for both sexes in the engine) — a
   reminder that the male survival value is **not** the female value and that the
   field range for male survival is wide (0.69–0.87 pooled in Epopa; see also
   `diallo-2026-continuous-mrr-mali.md`, where males and females had similar mean
   movement distances but no site fidelity).
3. **A male persists at a swarm site for days** (marked male recaptured in a swarm
   8 days after release). Swarm sites are therefore **persistent male microhabitats**,
   which supports the draft's static marker field.

**Caveats:** South African *arabiensis*, not *coluzzii*; very low recapture rates;
the pilot MRR was interrupted by 7-day collection windows; "swarms" are identified by
routine surveillance without detailed species-level swarm characterisation; the
dispersal figure is a mean over 7 marked males.
