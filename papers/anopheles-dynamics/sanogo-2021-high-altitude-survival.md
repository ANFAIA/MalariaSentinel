# The Effects of High-Altitude Windborne Migration on Survival, Oviposition, and Blood-Feeding of the African Malaria Mosquito, Anopheles gambiae s.l. (Diptera: Culicidae)

**Authors:** Zana L. Sanogo, Alpha S. Yaro, Adama Dao, Moussa Diallo, Ousman Yossi, Djibril Samaké, et al.
**Journal:** Journal of Medical Entomology | **Year:** 2021 (online 2020) | **DOI:** 10.1093/jme/tjaa137
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/sanogo-2021-high-altitude-survival.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

High-altitude windborne migration raised the question of whether transported
mosquitoes remain viable. Wild *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. were subjected to a
high-altitude survival assay followed by oviposition and blood-feeding assays.
Even under exceptionally harsh conditions, a high proportion survived assay
durations of 6 and 11 h at 120–250 m altitude, and egg-laying and subsequent
blood-feeding success differed only minimally from ground controls.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Windborne migrants can survive extended high-altitude flight, which is the
  biological basis for a non-zero survival term during wind migration
  (`dispersal.wind_survival`).
- The paper reports survival as a high proportion across assay durations, not
  a single fitted daily probability; the engine's fraction is an engineering
  prior anchored on this qualitative result.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Provenance for the survival side of the wind-migration parameters. Huestis
2019 establishes that windborne transport happens; this study establishes that
migrants remain viable after it.
