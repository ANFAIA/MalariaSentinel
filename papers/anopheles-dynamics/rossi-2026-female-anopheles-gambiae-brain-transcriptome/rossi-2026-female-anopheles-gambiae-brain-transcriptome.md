# The brain of the female *Anopheles gambiae* mosquito at spatial and single-cell resolution

**Authors:** I. Z. Rossi, C. Cassan, J. W. Bagnoli, S. Özhan, A. Valente, Q. J. Delorme, et al.
**Journal:** Nature Communications | **Year:** 2026 | **Volume:** 17:1 | **DOI:** 10.1038/s41467-026-76839-z
**Open access URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13680466/
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/rossi-2026-female-anopheles-gambiae-brain-transcriptome.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Spatial and single-cell transcriptomic atlas of the female *Anopheles gambiae*
brain (Slide-seq + scRNA-seq + bulk RNA-seq), including a comparison of
**age-matched sugar-fed mated females vs. mated females 24 h after a blood
meal**. Used here to test the specific claim that a blood meal causes
**down-regulation of sensory receptors, including visual opsins**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Experimental contrast: "We then compared the brains of age-matched sugar-fed
  mated females with those of mated females **24 h after a bloodmeal**".
  (Bulk RNA-seq, N = 1 replicate per condition, n = 29 single brains; scRNA-seq,
  N = 2 replicates, n = 60 pooled brains.)
- "Single brain differential expression analysis identified **1,895 DEGs**."
- "Blood feeding induced a profound alteration in genes associated with
  **translation activity**. This was evidenced by an overall **downregulation of
  genes encoding ribosomal proteins, translation initiation and elongation
  factors, and tRNA synthetases**, suggesting a decrease in translation processes
  in the brain following blood feeding."
- "A concomitant **downregulation of genes involved in cell proliferation and
  intracellular signaling (e.g., putative GPCR receptors, kinases of the MAPK
  cascade, Ser/Thr kinases, ion channels)** could indicate a **lowered response to
  stimuli, consistent with the refractory/resting interval after a bloodmeal**."
- "On the other hand, we saw **increased** expression of genes associated with
  energy metabolism, such as enzymes of the purine, pyrimidine, amino acid
  biosynthesis, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, the Krebs cycle, and
  oxidative phosphorylation after bloodmeal."
- Chemosensory marker: "we identified an odorant binding protein gene, **obp9
  (AGAP000278), strongly upregulated** following bloodmeal … This gene is a robust
  marker of neuropil-associated glia (Ag10)"; "The DEA per-cell cluster revealed
  a **drastic glial transcriptional switch after bloodmeal**".
- Visual system (context, not a blood-meal contrast): the atlas identifies
  "**103 candidate genes linked to the eye region** … peripheral opsins in the
  photoreceptor cell (e.g., *gprop8*) … five dominantly expressed opsins
  (*gprop1, 3, 4, 6, 8*) in photoreceptors (Ag15)"; "Opsins are key to
  photoreception and have been shown to have expanded in different dipterans
  **but their specific role is unclear in *Anopheles***".

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

- **Provides the mechanistic backing for a physiological "host-seeking gate"**:
  a blood meal produces a broad brain transcriptional downshift in signalling
  (GPCRs, kinases, ion channels) — "a lowered response to stimuli, consistent
  with the refractory/resting interval after a bloodmeal" — while energy
  metabolism rises. This supports modelling the postprandial state as a
  *physiological* state (not merely an elapsed-time timer).
- **Explicitly checks the "visual opsins are down-regulated" claim**: in this
  atlas the opsin genes are *unchanged/unresolved* in the blood-meal contrast
  (the reported DEG classes are translation, proteostasis, proliferation,
  signalling and metabolism); the opsin result here is an *expression map*, not
  a post-blood-meal down-regulation. Combined with Takken et al. 2001 (EAG
  suppression of **olfactory** responses at Christophers stage IV) and Uehara
  et al. 2026 (behavioural suppression), the defensible claim is: **olfactory /
  general responsiveness is suppressed after a blood meal; a specific
  down-regulation of visual opsins was NOT verified in the sources read for
  this review.**
- Caveats: laboratory *A. gambiae* (G strain), single blood meal, one
  24 h-post-meal time point, very small replicate numbers (N = 1 bulk, N = 2
  scRNA-seq) — the DEG counts are indicative, not precise effect sizes. No
  *An. coluzzii*.
