# Ecology of reproduction of *Anopheles arabiensis* in an urban area of Bobo-Dioulasso, Burkina Faso (West Africa): monthly swarming and mating frequency and their relation to environmental factors

**Authors:** Bimbilé Somda NS, Poda BS, Sawadogo PS, Gnankiné O, Maiga H, Fournet F, Lees RS, Bouyer J, Gilles J, Sanon A, Diabaté A, Dabiré KR
**Journal:** PLoS ONE | **Year:** 2018 (13(11):e0205966) | **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0205966
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/bimbile-somda-2018-arabiensis-swarming-mating-burkina-faso.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

One year of field observations (480 swarms) of *Anopheles arabiensis* in Dioulassoba,
an urban district of Bobo-Dioulasso, Burkina Faso: monthly swarming occurrence,
swarm duration, swarm size, number of mating pairs, time to first mating, and their
relationship to rainfall, humidity and sunset. Also reports the **insemination and
blood-feeding status of females caught in copula** — i.e. whether mating precedes or
follows the first blood meal.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Swarm size:** mean **118.55 ± 0.65 mosquitoes per swarm** (N = 480 swarms),
  range **4–700**; swarm size depends on month (GLNM, df = 11, Wald χ² = 401.977,
  P < 0.001). Smallest swarms in **August–September** (peak rainfall) and April;
  largest in **November** and **March**.
- **Swarm duration:** mean **28.31 ± 0.35 min**; monthly means range 19–39 min.
- **Swarming time tracks sunset seasonally:** start and end times correlate with
  sunset (r = 0.934 and 0.980, both P < 0.001); swarming occurs **after or at sunset
  from late July to early October** and **before sunset from late October to early
  July** (i.e. the sun-set anchor moves with the season, the clock-time does not).
- **Mating:** **10 550 mating pairs** observed overall, **up to 287 pairs per swarm**;
  monthly means of pairs per swarm fall from ~80 (March) to **0 in August and
  September** (GLNM, df = 11, Wald χ² = 95.123, P < 0.001).
  *Paper-internal inconsistency to note:* the running text states "a mean of
  118.55 ± 6.05" pairs per swarm, which duplicates the swarm-size mean; the monthly
  marginal means (Table 6) average ≈ 28. The **within-paper inconsistency is flagged
  here rather than resolved**.
- **Swarm size predicts mating success strongly:** Pearson **r = 0.747**
  (P < 0.001, N = 458) between number of mating pairs and swarm size; swarming
  duration correlates moderately with both (r = 0.426 and 0.421).
- **Rain suppresses reproduction:** the higher the rainfall and rain frequency, the
  lower the swarming duration, swarm size and especially the number of mating pairs.
- **Time from swarm formation to first mating:** **2–35 min, mean 13.32 ± 0.37 min**
  (N = 310).
- **Mating precedes feeding:** of **2 316 mating pairs collected**, only **68
  (2.97 %)** of the females caught in copula were blood fed, and of **1 439 females
  dissected, 1 417 (98.47 %) were inseminated**.
- Males caught in copula and males caught solo did not differ in body size or in the
  proportion that had taken a recent sugar meal.
- No *P. falciparum* was detected in the females examined.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This is the best *West-African, same-region* measurement set for **arabiensis** swarm
phenology, and the closest available proxy for the *coluzzii* swarm of the Ghana pilot
(→ evidence tier **[P]**).

- It supplies a **complete temporal parameterisation of the swarm window** that the
  Centinela's time phases (DAY/EVENING/NIGHT/DAWN) could host: swarms start within
  ±30 min of sunset, last ~28 min, and the first mating occurs ~13 min into the swarm.
- It shows that **mating is essentially always pre-gravid / pre-blood-meal**
  (2.97 % of copulating females had fed) → the male module, if it exists, must act
  on the **teneral** female, before host seeking, not on the gravid female.
- It shows that **swarm availability is seasonal and rainfall-driven**, i.e. it is a
  *spatially and temporally structured* quantity, not a constant. A constant
  male:female ratio + constant insemination probability would erase this.
- **Caveats:** *A. arabiensis* (urban, Burkina Faso), not *A. coluzzii*; swarm size is
  the count of *mosquitoes* (both sexes) per swarm, not males per swarm; the pairs-per-
  swarm mean is internally inconsistent in the paper.
