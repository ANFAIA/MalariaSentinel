# Estimating dispersal and survival of Anopheles gambiae and Anopheles funestus along the Kenyan coast by using mark-release-recapture methods

**Authors:** J. T. Midega, C. M. Mbogo, H. Mwambi, M. D. Wilson, G. O. Ojwang, J. M. Mwangangi, J. G. Beier
**Journal:** Journal of Medical Entomology | **Year:** 2007 | **DOI:** 10.1603/0022-2585(2007)44[923:EDASOA]2.0.CO;2
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/midega-2007-anopheles-kenyan-coast-mrr.md

## Abstract

Mark-release-recapture (MRR) experiments were conducted with emerging *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. and *Anopheles funestus* Giles at Jaribuni and Mtepeni in Kilifi, along the Kenyan Coast. Of 739 and 1,246 *Anopheles* released at Jaribuni and Mtepeni, 24.6 % and 4.33 % were recaptured, respectively. **The daily survival probability was 0.96 for *An. funestus* and 0.95 for *An. gambiae* in Jaribuni and 0.83 and 0.95, respectively, in Mtepeni.** The maximum flight distance recorded was 661 m. The high survival probability of *An. gambiae* and *An. funestus* estimated accounts for the continuous transmission of malaria along the Kenyan coast.

## Methods

- **Sites:** Jaribuni and Mtepeni, Kilifi District, Kenyan Coast (perennial malaria transmission).
- **Release:** Emerging (3-d-old) *Anopheles* females marked at their larval habitat — a methodological improvement over previous MRR studies that released older, host-seeking females.
- **Recapture:** Indoor resting collections for 2 weeks post-release.
- **Survival model:** Gillies (1961) exponential model: log-linear regression of recapture counts on days post-release; slope's antilog = daily survival probability.

## Key Results

- **Daily survival probability (Jaribuni):** *An. gambiae* s.s. = **0.95** (95 % CI 0.88–1.00); *An. funestus* = **0.96** (95 % CI 0.89–1.00).
- **Daily survival probability (Mtepeni):** *An. gambiae* s.s. = **0.95**; *An. funestus* = **0.83**.
- **Combined range across sites and species:** **0.83–0.96/day**.
- **Maximum flight distance:** 661 m (consistent with Thomas et al. 2013 Gambia range).
- **Mean dispersal distance:** Not directly reported, but the recapture distribution supports sub-kilometer daily movement.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This paper provides **the upper bound for the field-validated daily survival range** for *An. gambiae* (0.95 at the Kenyan coast). The Centinela's `ADULT_DAILY_MORT_BASAL = 0.93` is **consistent with this value** and with the lower-bound estimates from Costantini 1996 (0.80–0.88) and Saarman 2019 (0.87). The use of emerging (3-d-old) females in this study is methodologically important because it avoids the bias from releasing host-seeking females that may have already lost some of their initial mortality risk — a practice the Centinela could mirror in any future validation work.

For Ghana-specific calibration, the combination of Costantini 1996 (Burkina Faso savanna, low end), Midega 2007 (Kenya coast, high end), and Saarman 2019 (West Africa, mid-range) brackets the appropriate daily survival range.

## Limitations

- Coastal Kenya is ecologically distinct from West African savanna; survival may differ due to humidity, predation, and house-screening practices.
- Indoor resting collections underestimate exophilic *An. arabiensis*.
- The 3-d-old release protocol is methodologically superior but means the survival estimate covers only the post-emergence phase, not the teneral period.
- Two sites only; spatial replication is limited.

## Future Directions

- Combine emerging-female MRR with the self-marking unit method (Saarman 2019) to cover all age classes.
- Extend to insecticide-resistant populations (which may have lower survival costs of resistance).
- Validate the Centinela's Ghana ABM against multi-site MRR data when available.
