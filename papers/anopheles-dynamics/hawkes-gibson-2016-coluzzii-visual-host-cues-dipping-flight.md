# Seeing is believing: the nocturnal malarial mosquito *Anopheles coluzzii* responds to visual host-cues when odour indicates a host is nearby

**Authors:** Frances Hawkes, Gabriella Gibson
**Journal:** Parasites & Vectors | **Year:** 2016 (9:320) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s13071-016-1609-z
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/hawkes-gibson-2016-coluzzii-visual-host-cues-dipping-flight.md
**Copy read:** publisher PDF (version of record) — https://d-nb.info/1102740020/34

## Abstract (summary)

3-D video tracking of individual host-seeking ***Anopheles coluzzii*** females
in a laboratory wind tunnel, in two experiments: (i) flight behaviour in the
vicinity of a live human host vs clean air ("ranging" vs "host location"
flight, within ~1.5 m of the host); (ii) response to a visually conspicuous
object (black vs transparent tile) in clean air vs with host odour.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

### Apparatus and conditions  [M]
* Colony: *An. coluzzii*, M-molecular form of *An. gambiae* s.s., from
  IRSS/Centre Muraz, Bobo-Dioulasso, Burkina Faso; 26 ± 2 °C, 70 ± 5 % RH,
  12:12 L:D.
* Flight arena 1.2 m wide × 1.2 m tall × 2 m long; **airflow 0.1 m s⁻¹**;
  25 ± 2 °C, 65 ± 5 % RH.
* **Light: ~1.16 W m⁻²** white LED array (420–680 nm) on the arena floor —
  the authors state this is "similar in intensity to natural starlight
  reflected off the ground". IR LEDs (840 nm) back-light for the cameras.
* Host = female volunteer seated in an odour-delivery chamber behind a
  mosquito-net screen; mouth ~35 cm above the floor; **extra humidified CO2
  at 4.8 % released at 5 l min⁻¹** next to her mouth; host odour field
  "~60 cm wide at floor level to ~20 cm wide at the volunteer's head"; coherent
  breath+CO2 plume "mean height of ~30 cm and ~30–50 cm in diameter".
* **CO2 field: highest concentrations 1,400–2,000 ppm at ~30 cm height
  ~50 cm downwind of the host; mean plume-centre concentration 1,060 ± 30 ppm
  (range 791–3,462 ppm); background CO2 460 ± 5 ppm (439–511 ppm).**
* 3-D capture volume ≈ 60 cm wide × 85 cm long, upwind edge ~20 cm from the
  upwind screen (so recorded mosquitoes were within ~1 m of the host).
* **Sampling rate: "3D flight coordinates of only one mosquito at a time were
  recorded … (recording rate 50 Hz by TrackIt3D software)".** The paper's
  cumulative horizontal displacement is computed "**per 20 ms intervals**",
  i.e. the empirical integration step of this study is **1/50 s**.
* Visual target: 20 × 20 cm IR-transmitting tile held upright ~15 cm above the
  floor, **~55 cm downwind of the host**, perpendicular to the wind.
* Experiments in the **first 3 h of the scotophase**; sugar withdrawn 3 h
  before the trial; 5 females per trial, 10 min per trial.
* The authors explicitly describe the within-2 m odour field as **not** a
  narrow laboratory plume: "the odour plume is better described as a broad
  field of host-odour laden air", and note that "broken-up packets of host
  odour" are what would be encountered **"> 1 m downwind of the host"**
  (citing Murlis & Jones 1981, *Physiol. Entomol.* 6:71–86).

### Flight-speed / kinematics by flight regime (ground speed)  [M]

| Regime | Ground speed | Mean angular velocity | Tortuosity | Mean height, clean air → host odour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smooth | **45.1 ± 1.63 cm s⁻¹** | **268.5 ± 33.03 ° s⁻¹** | 0.9 ± 0.03 | **11.8 ± 0.22 cm → 13.0 ± 0.29 cm** (+1.2 cm) |
| Tortuous | **29.0 ± 2.57 cm s⁻¹** | **497.8 ± 52.22 ° s⁻¹** | 0.3 ± 0.04 | **7.5 ± 0.20 cm → 13.6 ± 0.13 cm** (+6.1 cm) |
| Dipping | **22.5 ± 1.14 cm s⁻¹** | **531.9 ± 25.71 ° s⁻¹** (turning almost exclusively in the vertical plane) | 0.8 ± 0.02 | **3.2 ± 0.15 cm → 3.4 ± 0.16 cm** (never > 3.5 cm) |

Track-type mix: clean air 29.0 % smooth / 11.6 % tortuous / 59.4 % dipping;
with host odour 14.3 % / 34.7 % / 51.0 % (χ² = 10.28, df = 2, P = 0.006).
Mean track duration 1.2–1.9 s (smooth), 3.6–3.9 s (tortuous), 1.3–1.9 s
(dipping).

Dipping geometry (host odour): trough minima only **0.2 ± 0.06 cm** above the
ground; broad peaks ~4.0 cm above troughs; mean dip amplitude 2.8 ± 0.1 cm
(host) vs 1.9 ± 0.1 cm (clean); trough-to-trough period 0.51 ± 0.02 s (host)
vs 0.43 ± 0.02 s (clean); mean descent angle **26.0 ± 2.03°**; sharp upward
turn **70.5 ± 2.48°** at the trough; steep ascent **50.7 ± 2.71°** for the
first 0.1 s; exit angle 26.4 ± 2.75°.

### Odour-gated visual response (experiment 2)  [M]

| Tile | Clean air | Host odour |
|---|---|---|
| Black (high contrast) | **9.8 %** of tracks responded | **43.4 %** |
| Clear (transparent) | **6.8 %** | **24.1 %** |

Difference black vs clear with odour: Fisher's exact P < 0.05. Clean air black
vs clear: not different (P = 0.721). Author's conclusion: "This indicates in
*An. coluzzii* an **odour-dependent responsiveness to visual stimuli**;
without host odour they did not respond to visual cues."

"Responder" definition (a published, usable behavioural criterion): the
mosquito flew toward the tile, came within **0–15 cm of the tile's surface**,
and executed a change in angular velocity of **> 90°** at its closest point.
Mean turning distance from the black tile **5.4 ± 0.85 cm**; from the clear
tile 4.5 ± 1.13 cm (no significant difference, P = 0.5).

Flight control during the visual response: ground speed **decelerates from
40 cm s⁻¹ to 30 cm s⁻¹ in the 0.1 s** before closest approach, then
accelerates at ~80 cm s⁻² to a mean 46 cm s⁻¹ within 0.1 s of moving away;
mean angular acceleration **12,500 ° s⁻²** between 0.04 s before and 0.04 s
after closest approach. **The turning event itself is therefore resolved on a
~0.08 s window (4 frames at 50 Hz)** — the fastest behavioural feature
documented in this paper.

### Activation  [M]
54.5 % of mosquitoes flew out of the release cage in clean air vs **78.1 %**
with host odour (χ² = 34.1, df = 1, P < 0.001).

### Navigation mechanism stated by the authors  [P]
The paper frames the long-range mechanism as "odour-mediated **opto-motor
guided positive anemo-taxis**, the mechanism by which mosquitoes fly toward a
host from a distance" — the mosquito infers wind direction from the optical
flow of ground features and steers upwind inside the plume.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This is the **primary source for the *A. coluzzii*-specific values** in the
movement law:

* `visual_radius` — the odour-gated visual response is demonstrated at
  **≤ ~1 m from the host** (the 3-D capture volume extended ~1 m downwind of
  the host; the tile stood ~55 cm downwind of the host, and the mosquitoes
  that responded closed to **0–15 cm** of the tile, mean 5.4 cm). The "≤1.5 m"
  figure used in the synthesis is the **arena/host-proximity envelope**, not a
  measured visual detection radius — it should be treated as such. **[M]**
* `visual_response_rate` — 9.8 % without odour vs 43.4 % with odour: this is
  the quantitative evidence that the visual vector must be **gated on the
  olfactory state**; an ungated visual attraction term would over-attract by
  ~4×. **[M]**
* `flight_speed` — the three named regimes (smooth / tortuous / dipping) and
  their **ground** speeds (45.1 / 29.0 / 22.5 cm s⁻¹) and angular velocities
  (268.5 / 497.8 / 531.9 ° s⁻¹) are the numbers the movement law should use
  for host-seeking females. **Note these are GROUND speeds in a 0.1 m s⁻¹
  laminar flow, not airspeeds** — for an upwind track the airspeed is
  ≈ ground speed + 0.1 m s⁻¹. **[M]**
* `flight_altitude` — the host-seeking flight band is **11–14 cm** for
  smooth/tortuous tracks and **< 3.5 cm** for dipping, i.e. the mosquito is
  in the surface boundary layer, which is also why the wind at the mosquito's
  own altitude is far below the free-stream wind. **[M]**
* `dipping` should be modelled explicitly as the low-altitude near-ground
  regime, since 51–59 % of all host-seeking tracks are dipping regardless of
  odour. **[M]**

**Caveats:** laboratory wind tunnel, one colony, one volunteer host, Burkina
Faso origin; single-mosquito 3-D tracking; the host's body heat was largely
dissipated by the screen and by the >20 cm stand-off, so the visual
experiment is deliberately *not* a heat + odour experiment.
