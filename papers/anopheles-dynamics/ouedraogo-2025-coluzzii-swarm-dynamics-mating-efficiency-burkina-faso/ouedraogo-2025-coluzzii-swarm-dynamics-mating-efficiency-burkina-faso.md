# Characterization of factors influencing swarm dynamics and mating efficiency in *Anopheles coluzzii*

**Authors:** Ouédraogo TFX, Sawadogo SP, Millogo AA, Niang A, Ouedraogo J, Ouattara SB, Cribellier A, Namountougou M, Dabiré RK, Muijres FT, Diabaté A
**Journal:** Parasites & Vectors | **Year:** 2025 (18:512) | **DOI:** 10.1186/s13071-025-07151-w
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/ouedraogo-2025-coluzzii-swarm-dynamics-mating-efficiency-burkina-faso.md

## Abstract (summary)

Field survey of *Anopheles coluzzii* mating swarms in the Vallée du Kou (VK5 district,
Bama village, western Burkina Faso), a rice-growing Sudanian-Sahelian area: 40
residential compounds ("compounds" = household units) were surveyed for swarms,
georeferenced by GPS, with swarm height, timing and (in three compounds) swarm size,
mating-pair counts and inter-swarm distances measured by time-lapse photography.
The study asks which measured quantities determine swarm size and mating success,
i.e. it is the **first field quantification of the coluzzii swarm as a spatial
object** at compound scale.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Timing and duration:** swarming activity began between **6:05 PM and 6:45 PM**
  and dispersal occurred from **6:35 PM to 7:15 PM**; **average swarm duration ≈ 27 min**.
- **Swarm height:** **2.87 ± 0.20 m** above ground level (mean ± SE).
- **Swarm density per compound:** **169 swarms across 40 compounds**, i.e. **3–12 swarms
  per compound**.
- **Swarm size (photographic counts, both sexes):** **83 to 2 783 mosquitoes per swarm**;
  **mating pairs per swarm 7 to 693**.
- **Swarm spatial distribution is NOT random:** Moran's Index **0.2, P < 0.001**;
  Getis-Ord General G observed **0.008763** vs expected **0.003915**, Z = 4.349,
  **P < 0.000014** → swarms form **hot spots**; all swarms were in open areas without
  overhead obstacles, and 3-D visualisation shows swarms form **below tree canopy**
  (sheltered from wind, open horizon toward dusk luminosity).
- **Inter-swarm distance within a compound:** compound 21 **11.32 m** mean; compounds 6
  and 15 **18.14 m** and **18.88 m** respectively.
- **Swarm size is the strongest predictor of mating success:** GLMM, total mating pairs
  increase with swarm size (**t = 9.16, P < 0.001**), but **individual mating
  efficiency (pairs per swarming male) DECLINES with swarm size** → density-dependent
  competition; the authors note a practical ceiling of ≈ **1 000 mosquitoes** per swarm
  beyond which observed pairs may be under-counted.
- **Male body size does not matter much:** wing length 2.82 ± 0.18, 2.77 ± 0.15,
  2.78 ± 0.13 mm in the three compounds; **no significant relation between male wing
  length and swarm size (t = 1.868, P = 0.069)** and **no significant effect of wing
  length on total pairs (t = 1.49, P = 0.14)**, only a marginal positive association
  with per-male efficiency.
- **PCA:** the first two components explain **65.3 %** of variance; Dim1 is loaded on
  the biological traits (swarm size, mating pairs, wing length, male and female
  densities), Dim2 on the environment; **luminosity is negatively correlated** with
  the biological parameters (reduced swarm activity at low light).
- **Mating markers (discussion, from other sources):** swarms form above **specific
  markers** — wood bundles, rubbish piles, wells (i.e. the marker set reported by
  Diabaté 2011 and Niang 2021).

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This is the **best available coluzzii-specific field characterisation of the swarm as a
spatial object** → evidence tier **[M]** for the quantities above (species measured
directly, in West Africa, in a rice-growing setting comparable to the Ghanaian pilot).

It changes three things in the plan:

1. **It supplies the coluzzii values for the quantities the draft currently takes from
   *A. gambiae* or *A. arabiensis***: swarm height **2.87 m** (vs 1–3 m from Diabaté
   2011), swarm duration **≈27 min** (vs 28.3 ± 0.35 min, Bimbilé Somda 2018),
   inter-swarm spacing **11–19 m** (vs median nearest-neighbour 10 m, Diabaté 2011).
2. **It confirms that swarm size is the driver of mating but that per-male efficiency
   falls with swarm size.** That is a *non-linear* relationship between the proposed
   "swarm marker density" free number and the insemination rate, and the D5 slot formula
   (which is linear in males available) reproduces only the first half of it.
3. **It gives the spatial statistic the aggregate option actually needs**: the number of
   swarms per compound (3–12) and their clustered (non-random, hot-spot) distribution.
   An aggregate over a 1 km cell therefore cannot use a uniform density: swarms are
   point objects inside a compound, with a mean spacing of 11–19 m.

**Caveats:** western Burkina Faso rice-growing Sudan-Savannah, not coastal Ghana;
swarm sizes are counts of **mosquitoes of both sexes**, not males; swarm size was
obtained from 5-min-interval flash photographs; the ≥1 000-mosquito "ceiling" is a
stated observation-bias concern, not a measured saturation; the paper does not report
the number of **females** per swarm, only total mosquitoes.
