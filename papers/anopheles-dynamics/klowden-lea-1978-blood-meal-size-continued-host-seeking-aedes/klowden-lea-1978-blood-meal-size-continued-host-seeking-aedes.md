# Blood meal size as a factor affecting continued host-seeking by *Aedes aegypti* (L.)

**Authors:** Michael J. Klowden, A. O. Lea
**Journal:** The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene | **Year:** 1978 | **DOI:** 10.4269/ajtmh.1978.27.827
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/klowden-lea-1978-blood-meal-size-continued-host-seeking-aedes.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Laboratory study (olfactometer) in which *Aedes aegypti* females were fed *partial* blood
meals; the host-seeking response within 1 h of the meal was then scored and the blood meal
size was measured chemically immediately afterwards. The paper establishes that the
volume of blood ingested, not merely the fact of feeding, determines whether the female
stops looking for hosts.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- "The suppression of host-seeking within 1 h after a blood meal appears to be caused by
  **abdominal distention from ingested blood**."
- "Mosquitoes of either strain were **attracted to a host when the blood meal size was
  less than 2.5 microliter**; above this threshold there was a sharp decline in the
  tendency to respond." → the explicit **2.5 µL** switching threshold between "keep
  seeking" and "stop seeking" (for *Aedes aegypti*).
- "**Small mosquitoes resulting from a low larval diet had a lower threshold**, and were
  more likely to cease host-seeking after a small blood meal." → the threshold is
  body-size dependent, i.e. it can be expressed relative to a size metric.
- "**Multiple feeding within a single gonotrophic cycle may result if mosquitoes take
  small blood meals which are insufficient to terminate host-seeking.**"
- "Partial meals and reduced feeding success of mosquitoes can result from **defensive
  host behavior**, which in the laboratory rat was shown to increase at high mosquito
  densities."

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

This is the source of the quantitative "meal size threshold" idea that the redesign
generalises, and of the mechanistic reason for the threshold (abdominal distension, not
a hormonal switch). It is an *Aedes* result: it is used here as an explicit **proxy** and
as the origin of the 2.5 µL number that the 2026 *Anopheles* literature also quotes as
the *Aedes* value (see `konopka-2026-…`). It supports making the threshold a parameter
rather than a boolean.

Caveats: 1978, *Aedes aegypti*, laboratory rat host, olfactometer; not an *Anopheles*
species and not a field measurement; the threshold is expressed in absolute µL for one
strain/size class.
