# Quantifying flight aptitude variation in wild Anopheles gambiae in order to identify long-distance migrants

**Authors:** Roy Faiman, Alpha S. Yaro, Moussa Diallo, Adama Dao, Samake Djibril, Zana L. Sanogo, Margery Sullivan, Asha Krishna, et al.
**Journal:** Malaria Journal | **Year:** 2020 | **DOI:** 10.1186/s12936-020-03333-2
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/faiman-2020-flight-aptitude-long-distance.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

The study measured flight aptitude of wild *Anopheles gambiae* from the
Sahel to identify individuals capable of long-distance migration, in a region
where surface water disappears for months and the population must be
re-established from elsewhere each year.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- Wild *An. gambiae* show variable but substantial flight capacity; the paper
  quantifies tethered flight duration and distinguishes resident from migrant
  phenotypes. It does NOT measure a survival fraction during windborne
  migration.
- The engine therefore uses this work for the flight-duration side
  (`dispersal.wind_flight_hours`); survival is anchored on Sanogo 2021 and an
  explicit engineering prior.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Provenance for the flight-aptitude/survival side of the wind-migration
parameters (Huestis 2019 covers the field evidence of windborne transport).
