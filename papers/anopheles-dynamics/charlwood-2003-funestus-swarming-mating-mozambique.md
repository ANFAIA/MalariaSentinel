# Observations on the swarming and mating behaviour of *Anopheles funestus* from southern Mozambique

**Authors:** Charlwood JD, Thompson R, Madsen H, Enserink M
**Journal:** Malaria Journal | **Year:** 2003 (2:2) | **DOI:** 10.1186/1475-2875-2-2
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/charlwood-2003-funestus-swarming-mating-mozambique.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Direct field observation of swarming, mating, house exiting and resting behaviour of
*Anopheles funestus* in Furvela, southern Mozambique, March–May 2002. The study asks
whether *An. funestus* and *An. gambiae* s.l. swarm in the same places, and reports
the **timing of mating relative to the first blood meal**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Swarm site:** "Mono-specific swarms occurred **at sunset in relatively open areas
  close to houses used for resting**." No obvious within-clearing pattern; at some
  sites two or more swarms formed within a few metres of each other.
- **Timing of mating inside the swarm:** "**Mating pairs were seen 11 ± 3.7 min after
  the start of swarming.**" The number of pairs observed was inversely proportional to
  the delay between swarm start and the first pairing.
- **Mating precedes feeding:** "**The great majority of females mated before
  feeding.**"
- **Swarms are mono-specific**, and the *marker* response is what isolates species:
  "In sharp contrast to the *An. gambiae* from São Tomé, **swarming males avoided
  black or white horizontal markers and dispersed when these were introduced under the
  swarm**"; *An. funestus* "avoided such markers (both light and dark) and dispersed
  if they were placed underneath naturally occurring swarms". "The response to
  potential horizontal markers is likely to be sufficient to prevent mixed swarms
  from occurring and this is likely to be the factor responsible for **isolation
  between *An. funestus* and *An. gambiae***."
- **Sex ratio inside swarms:** "the number of females entering swarms showed
  considerable variation"; the authors could not determine the number of males per
  swarm reliably. **601 males were collected from 13 swarms.**
- **Male reproductive capacity:** "One of the difficulties with this interpretation is
  that **a male can inseminate four or more females whilst females are largely
  monogamous**."
- Male and female body size did **not** affect mating success; female flight tone
  **516 Hz** vs **497 Hz** for the local *An. gambiae*.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

The key species-level lesson for a **per-species** male module: **swarm site
preference is species-specific and is mediated by the response to visual contrast
markers**, so a Centinela that hard-codes one swarm geometry for all anophelines
would be wrong. It also supplies two numbers the Centinela can use directly:

1. **mating happens ~11 min into a swarm that starts at sunset** (a phase-level
   timing constraint, compatible with the Centinela's EVENING phase);
2. **mating precedes the first blood meal**, i.e. the male acts on the teneral
   female — consistent with `charlwood-2003-mate-or-a-meal-pre-gravid-anopheles-gambiae.md`
   (*A. gambiae*: 49 % mated before feeding on the 2nd night, 51 % after).

**Caveats:** *An. funestus*, southern Mozambique, 2002, small number of swarms; the
"male can inseminate four or more females" statement is a discussion remark, not a
measurement; this is a **non-coluzzii** species → tier **[P]**.
