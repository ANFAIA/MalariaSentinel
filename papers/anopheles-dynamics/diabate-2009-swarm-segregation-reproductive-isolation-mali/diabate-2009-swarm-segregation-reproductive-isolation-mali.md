# Spatial swarm segregation and reproductive isolation between the molecular forms of *Anopheles gambiae*

**Authors:** Diabaté A, Dao A, Yaro AS, Adamou A, Gonzalez R, Manoukis NC, Traoré SF, Gwadz RW, Lehmann T
**Journal:** Proceedings of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences | **Year:** 2009 (276(1676):4215–4222) | **DOI:** 10.1098/rspb.2009.1167
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/diabate-2009-swarm-segregation-reproductive-isolation-mali.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Field study of the swarming behaviour of the **M and S molecular forms** of
*Anopheles gambiae* (now *A. gambiae* s.s. and *A. coluzzii*) in Donéguébougou and
Sokourani, Mali: 1 145 males were collected from 68 swarms at 19 sites in 2006 and
2007, and the question asked is whether **spatial segregation of swarms** is the
mechanism that restricts gene flow between the two incipient species. Answer: yes —
segregation is **virtually complete**.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Swarm structure (Donéguébougou):** swarms begin **2–5 min after sunset** with one
  or two males in zigzag flight; they last **20–40 min**; "Swarms remained stationary,
  flying within a **1.5 m radius** of an imaginary centre throughout their duration";
  **height 0.5–3 m** above ground, occasionally up to 4 m; **swarms were observed at
  the same sites repeatedly** (site fidelity).
- **Swarm composition:** **complete segregation** — in 2006, identification of 901 males
  from 47 swarms showed **every swarm composed exclusively of either M or S males**;
  in 2007, 20 of 21 swarms were pure. The expected number of mixed swarms under
  random mixing was "substantially higher than the number of mixed swarms observed
  (**P < 0.0001**)" in both years.
- **Indoors the forms are mixed:** 394 (2006) and 169 (2007) *A. gambiae* collected
  indoors (**45 % males, 55 % females**), with forms co-inhabiting houses near the
  swarms — so the segregation is a **swarm-site** effect, not a habitat effect.
- **Marker association is form-specific:** "All swarms of the **S form** were collected
  over **bare ground**, whereas the **M form** was strongly associated with markers
  consisting of **contrasting dark/light patterns**" (intersection of vegetation and
  footpath, a well surrounded by bare ground, a donkey cart, a chicken house, a wall on
  a lighter background); association between marker type and form **χ² = 56.92,
  df = 3, P < 0.0001**.
- **Mating couples:** 27 couples collected, **all homogeneously paired** (male and
  female of the same molecular form) and of the same form as the males of the swarm.
- **Female attendance experiment:** **455 tethered virgin females** introduced into
  94 M-form swarms; **47 (10.33 %)** were inseminated; **no significant difference
  between forms** (χ² = 2.38, df = 1, P = 0.122).
- Sample size per swarm 5–74 males, **median = 13**.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Evidence tier **[P]** for *A. coluzzii* (M form) and **[P]** for *A. gambiae* s.s.; the
species-pair structure (M/S) is the direct ancestor of the *coluzzii*/*gambiae* pair.

Three model-relevant consequences:

1. **A mechanism the project would otherwise miss:** swarm sites are **species-specific
   and marker-specific**, and the segregation is spatial (swarm-site) rather than
   habitat-based. A model with a single species-agnostic swarm-marker field and a
   single male pool cannot represent assortative mating between sympatric
   *coluzzii* and *gambiae*. For the Accra coastal AOI the species composition is
   *coluzzii*-dominated, so the practical risk is low — but the mechanism (per-species
   marker preferences) exists and is published.
2. **Swarm-site fidelity** ("swarms were observed at the same sites repeatedly") is the
   strongest published argument for treating the swarm marker as a **persistent
   spatial feature** (the draft's "swarm markers derived from the settlement pattern")
   rather than as a nightly random draw.
3. **The 10.33 % insemination of tethered virgin females released into M-form swarms**
   is one of the few measured **female-attendance-at-swarm** numbers available, and it
   is a *lower bound* on the per-female probability of being inseminated on arrival at
   a swarm of that size (median 13 males sampled per swarm).

**Caveats:** the M/S forms are not the *coluzzii*/*gambiae* sibling species exactly
(they are the ancestral subdivision of *A. gambiae* s.l.); tethered females are not
free-flying; the insemination rate is confounded with swarm size (median 13 males) and
with female age; the site-fidelity observation is qualitative.
