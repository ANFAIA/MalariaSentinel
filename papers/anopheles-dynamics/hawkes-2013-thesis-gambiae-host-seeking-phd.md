## Abstract (summary)

PhD thesis on the host-seeking behaviour of female *Anopheles gambiae*
(*An. gambiae* s.l. complex), using a purpose-built wind-tunnel arena with 3-D
video tracking to characterise "ranging" vs "host location" flight, the
kinematics of three flight types (smooth, tortuous, dipping), and the
odour-gated visual response. Includes an extensive literature synthesis of
odour-plume structure, plume-following and casting behaviour.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

### Plume structure: intermittency beyond ~1 m  [P]
The thesis reproduces the argument later published in Hawkes & Gibson (2016):
close to the host the stimulus is a **broad field** of host-odour-laden air,
but beyond ~1 m the odour arrives as **"broken-up packets"** of odour
(citing Murlis & Jones 1981, *Physiol. Entomol.* 6:71–86, "Fine-scale
structure of odour plumes in relation to insect orientation to distant
pheromone and other attractant sources"). This is the mechanistic reason why
a continuous concentration gradient is the wrong thing for an outdoor model to
follow.

### Casting / surge-and-cast model  [P]
* "*Ae. aegypti* behave in a way more akin to pheromone-tracking male moths,
  'casting' cross-wind upon leaving a plume and surging upwind when inside a
  plume (Dekker & Cardé, 2011)."
* On *Anopheles*: "As one would expect they are also able to fly upwind to
  follow the plume (Spitzen et al., 2008; Beeuwkes et al., 2008)" and, on the
  strategy, "Systematic horizontal 'casting' typical of moth flight
  (Baker, 1990; Cardé, 1996) may be inefficient in this context" because
  anthropophagic mosquitoes must find a large, variable, sometimes moving
  source.
* **Latency:** the thesis states that "*Ae. aegypti* also orientate more
  directly upwind **within 300 ms of entering** an odour plume from a human
  hand, then fly with a more crosswind bearing **300 ms after leaving** the
  same plume (Dekker et al., 2005)". **There is no "2 s" casting latency
  anywhere in this thesis, nor in Hawkes & Gibson (2016).** The nearest
  published latencies are the **300 ms** surge/cast times quoted here for
  *Ae. aegypti*.
* Geometric modelling (Sabelis & Schippers 1984, cited): "when mean wind
  variation is > 30°, up and downwind searching strategies are optimal; given
  that downwind flight is both a low energy means of movement and likely to
  result in plume entry at a point closer to the odour source than upwind
  flight, downwind orientation strategies may provide a valuable alternative
  means of seeking initial odour plume contact."

### Boundary layer  [P]
"In all directions, mosquitoes tend to fly within the atmospheric boundary
layer and **fly faster than the wind speed**, although they can be swept along
in the direction of the wind at higher elevation (Service, 1980)."

### Speeds inside vs outside the plume  [P]
Beeuwkes et al. (2008), on *An. gambiae* s.s. in a worn-sock odour plume
(14 of 245 females with sufficient plume contact): females **reduce their
flight speed and their track angle when inside an odour plume**, and flight
speed is reduced further as they approach the odour source; flight paths
outside the plume are "rather straight".

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Two structural justifications for the movement law, read from the thesis'
literature synthesis rather than from its own measurements:

1. **Casting is a moth-derived mechanism** that in *Anopheles* is described
   but, per this thesis, "may be inefficient in this context" because the host
   is a large, variable, mobile source rather than a point pheromone emitter.
   The Centinela should therefore treat casting as a *re-acquisition* device
   inside a bounded plume-interaction radius, not as the dominant long-range
   strategy. **[P]**
2. **The ≥ 30° wind-direction-variation result** (Sabelis & Schippers) gives a
   *published, testable* criterion for when to switch between upwind and
   downwind/crosswind plume-finding — a defensible alternative to an ad-hoc
   "casting timer". **[P]**

**Note:** this thesis contains **no measurement of a host-detection distance**.
The only latencies it reports are the 300 ms surge/cast times of
*Ae. aegypti*.
