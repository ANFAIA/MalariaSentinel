# Oviposition and Development of Anopheles coluzzii Coetzee and Wilkerson in Salt Water

**Authors:** E. K. Nwaefuna, Ibalafake Ibisobia Bagshaw, F. Gbogbo, M. Osae
**Journal:** Malaria Research and Treatment | **Year:** 2019 (vol. 2019, art. 9523962) | **DOI:** 10.1155/2019/9523962
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/nwaefuna-2019-oviposition-development-coluzzii-salt-water.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Laboratory experiments (insectary of the Radiation Entomology and Pest
Management Centre, Ghana Atomic Energy Commission, Nov 2014 – Mar 2015) on a
colony of *Anopheles coluzzii* founded in Nov 2014 from larval collections at
**Okyereko (05°24.960′N, 000°36.729″W), an irrigated rice village in the
Central Region of Ghana**. Test media were serial dilutions of seawater
collected at Nungua beach, Greater Accra (**100 % SW = 38.1 ppt**) from 0 to
100 % SW in 10 % steps. Oviposition preference, egg hatchability, larval
development (L3 and L4), pupation, adult emergence, starvation survival and
wing length were measured.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Oviposition:** females laid eggs at every salinity tested, but **~60 % of
  all eggs were laid in 0–11.7 ppt (0–40 % SW)**; the correlation between
  salinity and oviposition preference is significantly negative
  (**Pearson r = 0.54, p = 0.001**).
- **Egg hatchability:** **73 % in freshwater**, falling to **0.4 % at 30 % SW
  (10.5 ppt)**; **no hatching above 30 % SW**.
- **Larval mortality (LC50, log-dose-probit):** **L3 = 17.51 % SW (95 % CI
  9.31–24.56)**, **L4 = 23.4 % SW (95 % CI 16.76–22.30)**. No L3 survived in
  media with ≥40 % SW; the minimum concentration allowing L4 survival was
  **30 % SW (10.5 ppt)**.
- **Pupation:** L3 — 80 % in control, 70 % at 10 % and 20 % SW, ≤20 % at 30 %
  SW. L4 — pupation only up to 20 % SW (50 % at 10 % SW, 30 % at 20 % SW, 80 %
  in control).
- **Carry-over cost (energetic):** adults from L3 reared in freshwater or 10 %
  SW survived starvation **72 h**, versus **24 h** for those from 20–30 % SW;
  for L4, freshwater/10 % SW adults survived **96 h** vs 24 h at 20 % SW.
- Wing length of emerging adults did not differ significantly across salinities
  (2.81–3.08 cm).

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

The **only Ghana-specific measurement of the salinity response of *A. coluzzii***
covering oviposition, egg survival and larval development. It gives three
model-usable numbers: the oviposition salinity gradient (r = −0.54, 60 % of
eggs below 11.7 ppt), the egg-hatchability cut-off (~10.5 ppt), and the
larval LC50s (L3 17.5 %SW ≈ 6.7 ppt; L4 23.4 %SW ≈ 8.9 ppt). It is also the
empirical statement that **coluzzii can breed in brackish coastal lagoons and
estuaries of Ghana**, which is the premise of the `accra-coastal-test` AOI.
**Caveats:** single laboratory colony from one Central-Region rice village;
acute laboratory exposures; "% SW" and "ppt" differ (100 % SW = 38.1 ppt
here, so 30 % SW ≈ 11.4 ppt, while the paper quotes 10.5 ppt); the reported
L4 confidence interval (16.76–22.30) does not bracket the point estimate
23.4 — the paper's own numbers are internally inconsistent on that point.
