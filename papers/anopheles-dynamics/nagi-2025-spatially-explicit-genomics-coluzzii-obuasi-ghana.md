# Spatially-explicit genomics of *Anopheles gambiae* s.l. uncovers fine-scale population structure and mechanisms of insecticide resistance

**Authors:** SC Nagi, J Essandoh, N Ato-Egyir, ER Lucas, A Miles, K Desewu, I Williams, A Egyir-Yawson, L Sedda, D Weetman, MJ Donnelly
**Journal:** bioRxiv preprint | **Year:** 2025 (posted 13 June 2025) | **DOI:** 10.1101/2025.06.13.655782
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/nagi-2025-spatially-explicit-genomics-coluzzii-obuasi-ghana.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Whole-genome sequencing of **485 individual *Anopheles gambiae* s.l.** collected with a
spatially explicit sampling framework in **Obuasi, central Ghana** (an area with
extensive artisanal gold mining and documented multi-class pyrethroid resistance).
The paper addresses fine-scale **population structure** (i.e. how far apart you must
sample before the genetic signal changes) and characterises the **kinship** structure
of the local population.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- **Fine-scale structure in *A. coluzzii* is detected in Ghana:** "High resolution of
  fine-scale population structure enabled the detection of **isolation-by-distance in
  *An. coluzzii* in Obuasi**, whilst finding that at this scale, **geographic distance,
  rather than the underlying habitat, drives population structure**."
- **Kinship is measurable but noisy:** "We examine methods to estimate mosquito
  kinship, demonstrating that **polymorphic chromosomal inversions significantly
  confound established analytical tools**, while revealing how the mosquito chromosome
  architecture generates **high variance in genetic relatedness among kin**."
- **Resistance mechanisms:** novel mutations **Gste2-F120L** and **Cyp9K1-N224I** in
  detoxification enzymes under selective sweeps; resistance-variant frequencies
  associated with artisanal gold mines; continued evolution of the *Vgsc* sodium
  channel.
- Sample size: **485 individuals** of *An. gambiae* s.l., single locality.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Evidence tier **[M]** for the species and locality (*A. coluzzii*, central Ghana — the
Centinela's target country), but **[P]** for the trait of interest, because
**isolation-by-distance is a genetic, population-level signal, not a measurement of an
individual mosquito's behaviour**.

How to use it (and how not to):

- **What it does support:** the population-genetic structure of *A. coluzzii* in Ghana
  is **fine-scale and distance-driven**. Whatever the Centinela does with oviposition
  and dispersal, the resulting spatial pattern of breeding affiliation should
  reproduce **isolation-by-distance** (fine-scale IBD in the "isolation by distance"
  sense: nearby cells genetically similar, structure decaying with geographic
  distance, and *habitat* explaining less than *distance* at fine scale). This is a
  **model-level acceptance test**, and it is a Ghana-specific one — much better than
  the current West-African-savana proxy for the dispersal kernel.
- **What it does NOT support:** it says nothing about an individual female returning to
  her natal site. IBD is consistent with (i) philopatry, (ii) low dispersal combined
  with local selection, (iii) habitat-driven structure — the paper itself finds
  **distance, not habitat**, drives structure, which is the opposite of what a
  habitat-affinity model would produce and therefore a genuine discriminating test.
- **Warning on kinship-based philopatry inference:** the paper documents that
  chromosomal inversions **confound** kinship estimation and produce **high variance in
  relatedness among kin**. So any future attempt to estimate natal philopatry for
  *A. coluzzii* from kinship data is methodologically hazardous in this species.

**Caveats:** bioRxiv **preprint, not peer reviewed**; single locality (Obuasi) in a
**gold-mining** landscape, not the coastal AOI; 485 individuals is a modest sample
for fine-scale IBD; no explicit dispersal-distance estimate is quoted in the abstract
(the full text was not read for this extract).
