# Increased blood meal size and feeding frequency compromise *Aedes aegypti* midgut integrity and enhance dengue virus dissemination

**Authors:** Rebecca M. Johnson, David W. Cozens, Zeinab Ferdous, Patrick M. Armstrong, Douglas E. Brackney
**Journal:** PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases | **Year:** 2023 | **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pntd.0011703
**File:** papers/anopheles-dynamics/johnson-2023-blood-meal-size-feeding-frequency-aedes-midgut.md

---

## Abstract (summary)

Experimental study of the effect of *partial* blood meals on midgut basal-lamina damage
and arbovirus dissemination in *Aedes aegypti*. Motivated by the observation that wild
mosquitoes are frequently interrupted during feeding and take partial or minimal meals.

## Key Results (as used by the model)

- "*aegypti* provided blood meals **less than 2.5 μl continued to host-seek whereas larger
  blood meals blocked this behavior**." (citing Klowden & Lea 1978) → the 2.5 µL
  behavioural threshold, restated.
- "Another study reported that **32 % of engorged mosquitoes in Puerto Rico and 42 % in
  Thailand had taken double blood meals** while an additional **2 % and 5 %,
  respectively, had taken triple blood meals**." → field rates of multiple meals per
  gonotrophic cycle (Aedes; *Scott et al.* 2000 source).
- "While rates of **true partial feeding in field conditions are understudied** and degree
  of engorgement is often not scored, **partial feeding rates are likely high** due to host
  defensive behaviors and the well documented high levels of multiple blood meals."
- "**within 24 hours of feeding, damage was proportional to the number of blood meals
  imbibed**; however, this additive effect returned to baseline levels by 96 hours."
  → a mechanistic reason for treating consecutive meals as accumulating (and for a
  short-lived physiological carry-over window).
- "DENV-infected mosquitoes given a secondary **partial** blood meal had **intermediate**
  rates of dissemination and midgut basal lamina damage compared to single-fed and fully
  double-fed counterparts." → partial meals are not neutral: they carry epidemiological
  consequences in proportion to their size.

## Relevance to MalariaSentinel (Centinela)

Provides the epidemiological argument for keeping meal *size* (not just the number of
meals) in the model: the contribution of a meal to transmission scales with its volume.
It also flags, honestly, that the field rate of *true partial* feeding is essentially
unmeasured — which is exactly the quantity the redesign introduces.

Caveats: *Aedes aegypti* and dengue, used here as a proxy for the *Anopheles*/malaria
case; laboratory infections; the "32 %/42 %" multi-meal rates are Aedes field data and are
**not** the *Anopheles* rates (see `scott-2006-…`, `norris-2010-…`, `das-2017-…`).
